# Citrus Q&A: Yemen & Southern Saudi Arabia | أسئلة وأجوبة الحمضيات: اليمن وجنوب السعودية

### Q1: What are the main citrus-growing regions in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, and what makes each region suitable?

**EN:**

Citrus cultivation in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia spans several distinct agro-ecological zones, each offering particular advantages:

| Region | Country | Elevation | Climate Type | Key Advantage | Main Citrus Grown |
|--------|---------|-----------|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Lahj (Tuban Delta) | Yemen | 0–50 m | Hot coastal | Year-round warmth, alluvial soils | Acid lime, sweet orange |
| Abyan (Delta Abyan) | Yemen | 0–80 m | Hot coastal | Fertile wadi deposits, spate irrigation | Acid lime, lemon |
| Hadhramaut (Wadi Hadhramaut) | Yemen | 500–700 m | Arid inland valley | Deep alluvial aquifers, low humidity | Sweet orange, mandarin |
| Sana'a Basin | Yemen | 2,200+ m | Temperate highland | Cool winters for dormancy, low pest pressure | Mandarin, sweet orange |
| Tihama Plain | Yemen/Saudi | 0–50 m | Hot humid coastal | Long growing season, high heat units | Acid lime, grapefruit |
| Jazan (جازان) | Saudi Arabia | 0–400 m | Tropical coastal | Abundant groundwater, modern drip infrastructure | Lime, lemon, orange |
| Asir Highlands (عسير) | Saudi Arabia | 1,500–2,400 m | Semi-temperate | Cool nights improve fruit quality and color | Mandarin, sweet orange |
| Al-Baha (الباحة) | Saudi Arabia | 1,500–2,000 m | Semi-temperate | Terraced agriculture, adequate rainfall (250–400 mm) | Mandarin, lemon |

Lahj and Abyan together account for roughly 60% of Yemen's citrus output, while Jazan province dominates southern Saudi production. Highland areas (Sana'a, Asir, Al-Baha) produce smaller volumes but achieve superior fruit color and sugar-acid balance due to the diurnal temperature range (difference between day and night temperatures often exceeding 15°C).

### Q2: What are the best citrus varieties recommended for Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia?

**EN:**

Variety selection must account for heat tolerance, water salinity, market preference, and elevation. The following varieties have proven field performance across the region:

| Citrus Type | Variety | Local Name | Best Region | Maturity (months) | Key Trait |
|-------------|---------|------------|-------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Acid Lime | Mexican/Key Lime | ليم بلدي (Leem Baladi) | Lahj, Abyan, Tihama, Jazan | Year-round bearing | Heat tolerant, continuous fruiting |
| Acid Lime | Tahiti/Persian Lime | ليم فارسي | Jazan, Tihama | Year-round | Seedless, larger fruit, export quality |
| Lemon | Eureka | ليمون يوريكا | Hadhramaut, Asir | 8–10 | Good acidity, 4-season bearing |
| Lemon | Local Baladi Lemon | ليمون بلدي | Lahj, Abyan | 9–11 | Drought adapted, strong flavor |
| Sweet Orange | Valencia | برتقال فالنسيا | Hadhramaut, Asir, Al-Baha | 12–14 | Juice quality, late season |
| Sweet Orange | Washington Navel | برتقال أبو سُرّة | Sana'a, Asir | 10–12 | Seedless, fresh eating |
| Sweet Orange | Local Baladi Orange | برتقال بلدي | Lahj, Hadhramaut | 10–13 | Adapted to local conditions |
| Mandarin | Clementine | كلمنتين | Asir, Sana'a, Al-Baha | 8–10 | Easy peel, cold tolerant |
| Mandarin | Murcott (Honey Tangerine) | موركوت | Hadhramaut, Jazan | 10–12 | Very sweet, good shelf life |
| Mandarin | Baladi Mandarin | يوسفي بلدي | All regions | 9–11 | Locally adapted, seedy but flavorful |
| Grapefruit | Marsh Seedless | جريب فروت | Tihama, Jazan | 11–13 | Heat tolerant, juice market |
| Citron | Yemeni Citron | أُترُج يمني | Hadhramaut, Lahj | 10–12 | Traditional/medicinal use |

**Recommended rootstocks** (see also Q8 for salinity detail):
- **Volkamer Lemon** (الليمون الفولكاميري): Best for hot, saline coastal areas — vigorous, drought tolerant
- **Sour Orange** (النارنج): Traditional rootstock — excellent for Hadhramaut and highland areas, but susceptible to Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV)
- **Citrus macrophylla** (ماكروفيلا): Good vigor in hot climates, moderate salt tolerance
- **Citrumelo Swingle** (سيتروميلو سوينجل): CTV tolerant, suitable for highland replanting programs

### Q3: When is the best time to plant citrus trees, and what spacing should be used?

**EN:**

**Planting Time:**

| Region Type | Optimal Planting Window | Avoid Planting | Reason |
|-------------|------------------------|----------------|--------|
| Coastal (Lahj, Abyan, Tihama, Jazan) | October–February | May–August | Extreme heat (>42°C) causes transplant shock |
| Inland Valley (Hadhramaut) | November–January | June–September | Hot dry winds (>45°C) desiccate young trees |
| Highland (Sana'a, Asir, Al-Baha) | February–April | November–January | Frost risk below 0°C damages young grafts |

Container-grown (potted) nursery trees can extend the planting window by 4–6 weeks compared to bare-root stock, provided drip irrigation is available at transplant.

**Spacing Recommendations:**

| Citrus Type | Spacing (m) | Trees/Hectare | Canopy at Maturity | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|---------------------|-------|
| Acid Lime (Baladi) | 5 × 5 | 400 | 4–5 m diameter | Can use 4 × 4 with annual pruning |
| Acid Lime (Tahiti) | 6 × 5 | 333 | 5–6 m | More vigorous than Mexican lime |
| Lemon (Eureka) | 6 × 5 | 333 | 5–6 m | Semi-dwarf on Citrumelo: 5 × 4 |
| Sweet Orange | 6 × 6 | 278 | 6–7 m | Standard spacing |
| Sweet Orange (High-density) | 5 × 3 | 667 | Hedgerow pruned | Requires annual mechanical hedging |
| Mandarin | 5 × 5 | 400 | 4–5 m | Smaller canopy than orange |
| Grapefruit | 7 × 7 | 204 | 7–8 m | Largest canopy, needs space |

**Planting procedure:**
1. Dig hole 60 × 60 × 60 cm, two weeks before planting
2. Mix excavated soil with 20 kg well-decomposed organic matter + 500 g superphosphate
3. Plant graft union 10–15 cm above soil level (critical to prevent foot rot)
4. Create a basin (60 cm diameter) for irrigation
5. Apply 5–10 cm mulch layer (palm fronds, straw) keeping 15 cm clear of trunk
6. Irrigate immediately after planting (20–30 liters per tree)
7. Install shade cloth (50% shade) for first summer in coastal zones

### Q4: What are the irrigation requirements for citrus in arid and semi-arid conditions, and how does water quality affect production?

**EN:**

Citrus trees are sensitive to both water deficit and waterlogging. In the hot arid climate of Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, irrigation management is the single most critical factor determining orchard success.

**Annual Water Requirements by Region:**

| Region | Annual ET₀ (mm) | Citrus Crop Coefficient (Kc) | Annual Water Need (mm) | Liters/Tree/Year (mature) |
|--------|----------------|------------------------------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Coastal Tihama/Jazan | 2,000–2,400 | 0.65–0.75 | 1,300–1,800 | 18,000–25,000 |
| Lahj/Abyan | 1,800–2,200 | 0.65–0.75 | 1,170–1,650 | 16,000–23,000 |
| Hadhramaut (Wadi) | 2,200–2,600 | 0.60–0.70 | 1,320–1,820 | 18,000–25,000 |
| Sana'a Highland | 1,400–1,700 | 0.55–0.65 | 770–1,100 | 11,000–15,000 |
| Asir/Al-Baha Highland | 1,300–1,600 | 0.55–0.65 | 715–1,040 | 10,000–14,500 |

**Drip vs. Flood Irrigation Comparison:**

| Parameter | Drip Irrigation | Flood/Basin Irrigation |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Water use efficiency | 85–95% | 40–55% |
| Water saving vs. flood | 40–60% less water | Baseline |
| Salinity management | Excellent (continuous leaching) | Poor (salt accumulation between irrigations) |
| Root rot risk | Low (controlled moisture) | High (saturated root zone) |
| Weed pressure | Low (dry inter-rows) | High (wet surface) |
| Infrastructure cost | 3,000–5,000 SAR/ha | 500–1,000 SAR/ha |
| Labor requirement | Low (automated) | High (manual) |
| Recommended for | All regions, especially saline water | Highland areas with good water only |
| Emitter configuration | 4–8 emitters/tree (4 L/h), dual lateral | N/A |

**Critical Irrigation Periods** (do not allow water stress):
- Flowering (February–April): Deficit causes flower drop, up to 80% yield loss
- Fruit set (April–June): Deficit causes June drop, small fruit size
- Fruit expansion (June–September): Deficit reduces juice content and weight
- Pre-harvest (2–3 weeks before harvest): Slight deficit can improve sugar concentration

**Water Quality Thresholds for Citrus:**

| Parameter | Ideal | Acceptable | Problematic | Action Required |
|-----------|-------|------------|-------------|-----------------|
| EC (dS/m) | < 1.0 | 1.0–2.0 | 2.0–3.0 | Switch to salt-tolerant rootstock |
| SAR | < 3 | 3–6 | > 6 | Add gypsum, improve drainage |
| Chloride (meq/L) | < 4 | 4–10 | > 10 | Avoid overhead sprinkler, use drip |
| Boron (mg/L) | < 0.5 | 0.5–1.0 | > 1.0 | Blend with low-B water source |
| pH | 6.5–7.5 | 7.5–8.0 | > 8.5 | Acidify irrigation water |
| Bicarbonate (meq/L) | < 2 | 2–5 | > 5 | Acidify to prevent emitter clogging |

In Lahj and Abyan, groundwater salinity has been increasing (now commonly 2.0–4.0 dS/m) due to over-extraction. Using Volkamer Lemon or Cleopatra Mandarin rootstock combined with drip irrigation and a 15–20% leaching fraction is essential for sustained production under these conditions.

### Q5: What is the recommended fertilization program for citrus trees at different growth stages?

**EN:**

Citrus trees have high nutrient demands, particularly for nitrogen (N), potassium (K), and micronutrients (Zn, Fe, Mn). The fertilization program must be adjusted based on tree age, fruit load, soil type, and water quality.

**Annual Nutrient Requirements by Tree Age:**

| Tree Age (years) | N (g/tree) | P₂O₅ (g/tree) | K₂O (g/tree) | Organic Matter (kg/tree) | Zn (foliar sprays/year) |
|-------------------|-----------|----------------|--------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| 1–2 (establishment) | 50–100 | 30–50 | 30–50 | 10–15 | 1 |
| 3–4 (pre-bearing) | 150–250 | 75–120 | 100–200 | 15–20 | 2 |
| 5–7 (early bearing) | 300–500 | 150–200 | 250–400 | 20–30 | 2–3 |
| 8–15 (full production) | 600–900 | 200–300 | 500–800 | 30–50 | 3 |
| 15+ (mature) | 800–1,200 | 250–350 | 600–1,000 | 40–60 | 3 |

**Seasonal Fertilization Schedule for Bearing Trees (8+ years):**

| Period | Month | Fertilizer | Rate/Tree | Purpose |
|--------|-------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Pre-bloom | Jan–Feb | Ammonium Sulfate (21-0-0) | 1.0–1.5 kg | Vegetative flush, flower initiation |
| Bloom | Mar | Calcium Nitrate (15.5-0-0) | 0.5 kg | Fruit set, reduces flower drop |
| Post-set | Apr–May | NPK 20-10-10 | 1.0–1.5 kg | Fruit development |
| Fruit growth | Jun–Jul | Potassium Sulfate (0-0-50) | 1.0–1.5 kg | Fruit size and juice quality |
| Fruit maturation | Aug–Sep | Potassium Sulfate (0-0-50) | 0.5–1.0 kg | Sugar accumulation, rind color |
| Post-harvest | Oct–Nov | Superphosphate (0-46-0) + organic | 0.5 kg + 30 kg organic | Root growth, reserve buildup |
| Winter rest | Dec | None | — | Allow dormancy (highlands) |

**Micronutrient Foliar Spray Program:**

| Nutrient | Product | Concentration | Timing | Deficiency Symptom |
|----------|---------|--------------|--------|-------------------|
| Zinc (Zn) | Zinc Sulfate | 0.5% (5 g/L) | Feb, May, Aug | Interveinal chlorosis, small leaves (تقزم الأوراق) |
| Iron (Fe) | Iron Chelate (EDDHA) | 0.3% (3 g/L) | Mar, Jun | Young leaf yellowing (اصفرار الأوراق الحديثة) |
| Manganese (Mn) | Manganese Sulfate | 0.3% (3 g/L) | Mar, Jun | Interveinal chlorosis, pale bands |
| Boron (B) | Borax | 0.1% (1 g/L) | Feb (pre-bloom) | Fruit cracking, poor set (تشقق الثمار) |
| Copper (Cu) | Copper Oxychloride | 0.2% (2 g/L) | Dec (dormant) | Dieback, gum exudation |

**Fertigation Program (drip irrigation systems):**

For drip-irrigated orchards, split the total annual fertilizer into weekly applications through the irrigation system:
- N: 60% during spring-summer (Mar–Aug), 40% autumn-winter
- K: 70% during fruit growth-maturation (May–Sep)
- P: Apply 50% as basal soil application, 50% via fertigation
- Use soluble fertilizers: MAP (12-61-0), potassium nitrate (13-0-46), calcium nitrate (15.5-0-0)
- Maintain fertigation solution EC below 2.5 dS/m at emitter

**Important notes for the region:**
- Alkaline soils (pH > 7.5) in Hadhramaut and coastal areas lock up Fe, Zn, and Mn — foliar sprays are essential
- Ammonium-based fertilizers are preferred over nitrate in saline soils to counteract sodium uptake
- Avoid urea in high-temperature periods (>35°C) due to volatilization losses — use ammonium sulfate instead

### Q6: What are the major diseases affecting citrus in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, and how are they managed?

**EN:**

Citrus orchards in the region face several devastating diseases, with Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV), root rot (Phytophthora), and gummosis being the most economically significant.

| Disease | Pathogen | Local Name | Affected Parts | Severity | Key Regions |
|---------|----------|------------|----------------|----------|-------------|
| Citrus Tristeza | CTV (Closterovirus) | تريستيزا / الحزن | Whole tree (via rootstock) | Critical | All regions with sour orange rootstock |
| Phytophthora Root Rot | *Phytophthora citrophthora*, *P. nicotianae* | تعفن الجذور | Roots, crown, trunk base | High | Flood-irrigated areas (Lahj, Abyan) |
| Gummosis | *Phytophthora* spp. | التصمغ | Trunk, scaffold branches | High | Humid coastal areas |
| Citrus Canker | *Xanthomonas citri* | تقرح الحمضيات | Leaves, fruit, stems | Moderate | Jazan, Tihama (high humidity) |
| Sooty Mold | *Capnodium citri* | العفن الأسود | Leaf surface (on honeydew) | Low–Moderate | Where aphid/scale infestations occur |
| Mal Secco | *Plenodomus tracheiphilus* | الجفاف (مال سيكو) | Branches, leaves (lemon) | Moderate | Highland areas (Asir, Sana'a) |
| Greasy Spot | *Mycosphaerella citri* | البقعة الدهنية | Leaves (underside) | Low | Humid coastal orchards |
| Citrus Scab | *Elsinoë fawcettii* | جرب الحمضيات | Fruit rind, leaves | Low–Moderate | Mandarin, lemon in humid areas |

**Detailed Management for Top 3 Diseases:**

**1. Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV) — فيروس تريستيزا الحمضيات:**
- **Vector**: Brown Citrus Aphid (*Toxoptera citricida*) and other aphid species (مَنّ الحمضيات البني)
- **Symptoms**: Quick decline of trees on sour orange rootstock; stem pitting in sweet orange; seedling yellows in grapefruit and lemon
- **Management**:
  - Replace sour orange rootstock with CTV-tolerant alternatives (Citrumelo Swingle, Volkamer Lemon, Troyer Citrange)
  - Use certified virus-free budwood from approved nurseries
  - Control aphid vectors with imidacloprid (soil drench) or lambda-cyhalothrin (foliar)
  - Remove and destroy infected trees immediately to prevent spread
  - Cross-protection with mild CTV strains is practiced in some Saudi programs

**2. Phytophthora Root Rot and Crown Rot — تعفن الجذور والتاج:**
- **Conditions**: Waterlogged soils, flood irrigation, poor drainage, planting graft union below soil line
- **Symptoms**: Canopy thinning, pale leaves, root discoloration, bark peeling at crown, sudden tree collapse in severe cases
- **Management**:
  - Improve drainage; convert from flood to drip irrigation
  - Maintain graft union 15+ cm above soil line
  - Apply metalaxyl (Ridomil) as soil drench: 2 g/L around trunk base, twice per year (spring + autumn)
  - Phosphonate (fosetyl-Al) trunk injections for severely affected trees
  - Use tolerant rootstocks: Trifoliate orange, Citrumelo Swingle

**3. Gummosis (التصمغ):**
- **Pathogen**: Primarily *Phytophthora citrophthora* and *P. parasitica*
- **Symptoms**: Amber gum exudation from trunk and scaffold branches; bark cracking; lesions that girdle the trunk
- **Management**:
  - Surgically remove infected bark (cut 2 cm into healthy tissue)
  - Apply copper-based paste (Bordeaux paste) to wound
  - Paint trunks with white latex paint + copper hydroxide to prevent infection
  - Avoid trunk wetting from irrigation — use drip emitters 30+ cm from trunk
  - Maintain dry trunk base; remove soil mounding around trunk

**Preventive Orchard Hygiene Program:**

| Action | Frequency | Product/Method |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Copper spray (dormant) | Annual (December) | Bordeaux mixture 1% or copper hydroxide |
| Trunk whitewash | Annual (before summer) | White lime + copper sulfate 2% |
| Fallen fruit removal | Weekly during season | Collect and bury/compost away from orchard |
| Pruning wound seal | After each pruning | Copper paste or mastic sealant |
| Nursery inspection | At purchase | Verify CTV-free certification |
| Soil drainage check | Biannual | Ensure no waterlogging after irrigation |

**AR:**

تواجه بساتين الحمضيات في المنطقة عدة أمراض مدمرة، حيث يُعد فيروس تريستيزا الحمضيات (CTV) وتعفن الجذور (فيتوفثورا) والتصمغ الأكثر أهمية اقتصادية.

**الإدارة التفصيلية لأهم ٣ أمراض:**

**١. فيروس تريستيزا الحمضيات (CTV):**

  - مكافحة المَنّ الناقل بالإيميداكلوبريد (غمر تربة) أو لامبدا-سيهالوثرين (رش ورقي)

**٣. التصمغ:**
- **الأعراض**: إفراز صمغ عنبري من الجذع والأفرع الرئيسية؛ تشقق اللحاء؛ آفات تطوّق الجذع
- **المكافحة**:
  - إزالة اللحاء المصاب جراحيًا (قطع ٢ سم في النسيج السليم)
  - دهان معجون نحاسي (عجينة بوردو) على الجرح
  - طلاء الجذوع بدهان أبيض + هيدروكسيد النحاس لمنع الإصابة
  - تجنب ترطيب الجذع من الري — نقاطات على بعد ٣٠+ سم من الجذع
  - الحفاظ على جفاف قاعدة الجذع

### Q7: What are the major pests of citrus in the region, and what integrated pest management (IPM) strategies are recommended?

**EN:**

The warm climate of Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia supports year-round pest populations. An IPM approach combining biological control, cultural practices, and targeted chemical intervention is essential for sustainable citrus production.

| Pest | Scientific Name | Arabic Name | Damage | Severity | Peak Season |
|------|----------------|-------------|--------|----------|-------------|
| Citrus Leaf Miner | *Phyllocnistis citrella* | نافقة أوراق الحمضيات / صانعة الأنفاق | Serpentine mines in young leaves; distortion; entry point for canker | High | Mar–Oct (warm months) |
| Mediterranean Fruit Fly | *Ceratitis capitata* | ذبابة الفاكهة المتوسطية | Larvae feed inside fruit; premature drop; secondary rot | Critical | Jun–Nov (fruit maturation) |
| Citrus Aphids | *Toxoptera citricida*, *Aphis gossypii* | مَنّ الحمضيات | Sap sucking; leaf curl; honeydew/sooty mold; CTV vector | High | Feb–May, Sep–Nov |
| Scale Insects (Soft) | *Coccus hesperidum*, *Saissetia oleae* | الحشرات القشرية الرخوة | Sap sucking; honeydew; branch dieback | Moderate–High | Year-round |
| Scale Insects (Armored) | *Aonidiella aurantii* (California Red Scale) | الحشرة القشرية الحمراء | Fruit blemish; branch dieback; tree decline | High | Year-round |
| Citrus Red Mite | *Panonychus citri* | أكاروس الحمضيات الأحمر | Stippling; bronzing of leaves; reduced photosynthesis | Moderate | Hot dry periods (Jun–Sep) |
| Whitefly | *Dialeurodes citri* | الذبابة البيضاء | Sap sucking; honeydew; sooty mold | Moderate | Spring, autumn |
| Citrus Psylla | *Diaphorina citri* | سيلا الحمضيات | Vector for Huanglongbing (HLB/greening); stunting | Emerging threat | Feb–Apr, Sep–Nov |
| Citrus Bud Mite | *Aceria sheldoni* | أكاروس براعم الحمضيات | Distorted fruit (lemons); bud damage | Low–Moderate | Spring flush |
| Citrus Thrips | *Scirtothrips citri* | تربس الحمضيات | Scarring on young fruit rind | Moderate | Bloom–fruit set (Mar–May) |

**IPM Strategies by Pest Category:**

**Citrus Leaf Miner (نافقة الأوراق):**
- **Biological**: Release *Ageniaspis citricola* (parasitoid wasp) — established in some Saudi programs; *Cirrospilus* spp. as native parasitoids
- **Cultural**: Avoid excessive nitrogen that stimulates flush; prune to synchronize flush periods
- **Chemical (threshold-based)**: Abamectin 1.8% EC (0.5 mL/L) on new flush when >25% of shoots infested; imidacloprid soil drench for young trees
- **Timing**: Spray at first sign of new flush leaves, not after mines are visible

**Mediterranean Fruit Fly (ذبابة الفاكهة):**
- **Monitoring**: Deploy Trimedlure or Ceralure-based traps at 2 traps/hectare from May onward; threshold: 5 flies/trap/week
- **Biological**: Augmentative release of *Diachasmimorpha longicaudata* parasitoids where available
- **Cultural**: Collect and destroy all fallen fruit (bury 50+ cm deep or solar-heat treat); early harvest of susceptible varieties
- **Chemical**: Protein bait spray (GF-120) + spinosad — spot application on lower canopy; avoid blanket spray to protect pollinators
- **Mass trapping**: Hemispherical McPhail traps with torula yeast at 20 traps/hectare

**Aphids (المَنّ):**
- **Biological**: Conserve natural enemies — *Coccinella septempunctata* (ladybird), *Chrysoperla carnea* (lacewing), *Lysiphlebus* spp. (parasitoid); avoid broad-spectrum insecticides
- **Cultural**: Control ant populations (ants protect aphids from predators) using bait stations
- **Chemical (threshold)**: Treat when >20% of new shoots are colonized; spirotetramat (Movento) 0.5 mL/L or flonicamid; rotate chemistries to prevent resistance

**Scale Insects (الحشرات القشرية):**
- **Biological**: Release *Aphytis melinus* (parasitoid) for red scale; *Metaphycus* spp. for soft scale
- **Oil spray**: Mineral oil 1.5–2% during winter dormancy (December–January) to smother overwintering stages
- **Chemical**: Buprofezin (IGR) for crawlers; spirotetramat for systemic control
- **Monitoring**: Use sticky tape wraps on branches to detect crawler emergence

**General IPM Calendar:**

| Month | Monitoring Action | Treatment (if threshold reached) |
|-------|------------------|----------------------------------|
| Jan | Scale/mite check; dormant spray window | Mineral oil 2% + copper |
| Feb | Aphid scouting on new flush | Selective aphicide if >20% shoots |
| Mar–Apr | Leaf miner on flush; thrips at bloom | Abamectin on flush; spinosad at petal fall |
| May | Install fruit fly traps | Protein bait if 5+ flies/trap/week |
| Jun–Aug | Mite monitoring (leaf stippling) | Abamectin or sulfur if >30% leaves affected |
| Sep–Oct | Second aphid/whitefly wave; fruit fly peak | Targeted sprays; increase trap density |
| Nov–Dec | Scale assessment; plan dormant treatment | Winter oil spray; orchard sanitation |

### Q8: How do different citrus rootstocks compare in salinity tolerance, and which are recommended for saline conditions in the region?

**EN:**

Salinity is the most critical limiting factor for citrus production in lowland Yemen (Lahj, Abyan, Tihama) and coastal Saudi Arabia (Jazan), where groundwater EC commonly ranges from 2.0 to 5.0 dS/m. Rootstock selection is the primary management tool because citrus scions have limited inherent salt tolerance.

**Rootstock Salinity Tolerance Ranking:**

| Rootstock | Cl⁻ Exclusion | Na⁺ Exclusion | Max Irrigation EC (dS/m) | Vigor | CTV Tolerance | Phytophthora Tolerance | Recommended Region |

| Cleopatra Mandarin (يوسفي كليوباترا) | Excellent | Good | 3.5–4.0 | Low–Medium | Tolerant | Moderate | Lahj, Abyan, Jazan |
| Rangpur Lime (ليم رانغبور) | Very Good | Good | 3.0–3.5 | High | Tolerant | Low | Tihama, Jazan |
| Volkamer Lemon (ليمون فولكاميري) | Good | Moderate | 2.5–3.0 | Very High | Tolerant | Low–Moderate | All coastal regions |
| Sour Orange (النارنج) | Good | Good | 2.5–3.0 | Medium | **Susceptible** | Good | Hadhramaut only (no CTV pressure) |
| Citrus macrophylla (ماكروفيلا) | Moderate | Moderate | 2.0–2.5 | Very High | Tolerant | Low | Hot inland valleys |
| Citrumelo Swingle (سيتروميلو) | Low–Moderate | Low | 1.5–2.0 | Medium | Tolerant | Good | Highlands (good water) |

| Carrizo Citrange (كاريزو سيترانج) | Moderate | Low–Moderate | 2.0–2.5 | Medium–High | Tolerant | Good | Mid-elevation, moderate salinity |

**Chloride Toxicity Thresholds in Citrus Leaves:**

| Leaf Cl⁻ (% dry weight) | Status | Visible Symptoms |
|--------------------------|--------|-----------------|
| < 0.3% | Normal | None |
| 0.3–0.5% | Marginal | None visible, reduced growth |
| 0.5–0.7% | Moderate | Tip burn on older leaves |
| > 0.7% | Severe | Leaf necrosis, defoliation, yield collapse |

**Management Strategies for Saline Conditions:**

1. **Rootstock selection**: Use Cleopatra Mandarin or Rangpur Lime for water EC > 2.5 dS/m
2. **Drip irrigation**: Maintain continuous soil moisture to prevent salt concentration
3. **Leaching fraction**: Apply 15–20% excess water beyond crop ET to leach salts below root zone
4. **Organic mulch**: 10–15 cm layer reduces evaporation and surface salt accumulation
5. **Gypsum application**: 2–5 tons/hectare/year to displace sodium from soil exchange sites (for SAR > 6)
6. **Avoid overhead sprinklers**: Direct leaf contact with saline water causes severe leaf burn in citrus
7. **Soil amendments**: Elemental sulfur (500 kg/ha) for calcareous soils to lower pH and improve nutrient availability
8. **Monitoring**: Test leaf tissue chloride annually (sample October, non-fruiting terminals); test irrigation water EC monthly

**Rootstock Recommendation Decision Tree:**

| Irrigation Water EC | CTV Risk | Best Rootstock | Alternative |
|---------------------|----------|----------------|-------------|
| < 1.5 dS/m | Low (highland) | Sour Orange | Citrumelo Swingle |
| < 1.5 dS/m | High | Citrumelo Swingle | Troyer Citrange |
| 1.5–2.5 dS/m | Any | Volkamer Lemon | Carrizo Citrange |
| 2.5–3.5 dS/m | Any | Cleopatra Mandarin | Rangpur Lime |
| 3.5–4.0 dS/m | Any | Cleopatra Mandarin | Rangpur Lime (vigor boost) |
| > 4.0 dS/m | Any | **Not recommended for citrus** | Consider date palm or salt-tolerant crops |

**Harvesting Best Practices:**
1. Harvest in early morning (6–9 AM) when fruit temperature is lowest
2. Use clipper-cut (not pull) method — leave 1–2 mm stem button to prevent oleocell oil release and rind damage
3. Avoid harvesting wet fruit (dew or rain) — increases fungal risk
4. Use clean, sanitized field crates; never heap more than 3 layers of oranges
5. Transport to packhouse within 4 hours of harvest; shade during transport

**Post-Harvest Handling:**

| Step | Action | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1. Washing | Chlorinated water (100–200 ppm) dip or spray | Remove field debris, reduce microbial load |
| 2. Fungicide treatment | Imazalil (500 ppm) or thiabendazole (1000 ppm) dip | Prevent Penicillium (green/blue mold) |
| 3. Waxing | Food-grade shellac or carnauba wax | Reduce moisture loss, improve appearance |
| 4. Grading | By size (diameter) and blemish level | Market-specific sorting |
| 5. Packing | Ventilated cartons, 10–15 kg | Prevent compression damage |
| 6. Cold storage | 5–8°C oranges; 10–12°C limes/lemons; 85–90% RH | Extend shelf life 4–8 weeks |
| 7. Ethylene management | Degreening at 20–22°C with 1–5 ppm ethylene (2–3 days) for oranges if needed | Improve rind color for early season fruit |

### Q3: When is the best time to plant citrus trees and what spacing should be used?

**EN:**

**Planting time** depends on elevation and climate zone:

| Zone | Best Planting Window | Avoid Planting | Reason |
|------|---------------------|----------------|--------|
| Lowland hot zones (Lahj, Abyan, Jazan) | September–November | April–August | Extreme summer heat (>42°C) causes transplant shock |
| Mid-elevation (Hadhramaut, Najran) | October–December | May–August | Moderate winters favor root establishment |
| Highland (Sana'a, Asir, Al-Baha) | February–April | November–January | Frost risk in winter; spring warmth promotes growth |

**Spacing recommendations:**

| Citrus Type | Spacing (m) | Trees/Hectare | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|---------------|-------|
| Acid lime / Lemon | 5 × 5 | 400 | Can be closer (4 × 4) with intensive pruning |
| Sweet orange | 6 × 6 | 278 | Standard for rain-fed or drip-irrigated orchards |
| Sweet orange (high density) | 5 × 3 | 667 | Requires drip irrigation and annual hedging |
| Mandarin | 5 × 5 | 400 | Smaller canopy allows tighter spacing |
| Mandarin (high density) | 4 × 2.5 | 1,000 | Trellis-supported, intensive management |
| Grapefruit | 7 × 7 | 204 | Large canopy needs wider spacing |

**Planting procedure:**
1. Dig holes 60 × 60 × 60 cm at least 2 weeks before planting
2. Mix excavated soil with 20–30 kg well-decomposed farmyard manure + 500 g superphosphate per hole
3. Plant the budded seedling so the bud union is 15–20 cm above soil level (critical to prevent scion rooting)
4. Create a basin (60 cm diameter) around each tree for irrigation
5. Irrigate immediately after planting (30–40 liters per tree)
6. Apply 10 cm mulch layer to conserve moisture and reduce soil temperature
7. Provide shade (palm frond shelter) for the first summer in lowland zones

### Q4: What are the irrigation requirements for citrus in this region, and how does water quality affect production?

**EN:**

Citrus trees are relatively high water consumers compared to date palms or cereals. In the hot lowlands, annual water demand can reach 12,000–15,000 m³/ha. Drip irrigation is strongly recommended to conserve scarce water resources.

**Annual water requirements by zone:**

| Zone | Annual ET₀ (mm) | Crop Factor (Kc) | Annual Water Need (m³/ha) | Peak Monthly Need (m³/ha) |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Lowland (Lahj, Jazan) | 2,200–2,600 | 0.65–0.75 | 14,000–19,500 | 2,000–2,500 (Jun–Aug) |
| Mid-elevation (Hadhramaut) | 1,800–2,200 | 0.60–0.70 | 10,800–15,400 | 1,600–2,000 (May–Jul) |
| Highland (Sana'a, Asir) | 1,400–1,800 | 0.55–0.65 | 7,700–11,700 | 1,200–1,500 (Jun–Aug) |

**Drip irrigation vs. flood irrigation comparison:**

| Parameter | Drip Irrigation | Flood/Basin Irrigation |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Water use efficiency | 85–95% | 40–55% |
| Water savings vs. flood | 40–60% | Baseline |
| Salinity management | Excellent (leaches salts away from root zone) | Poor (salts concentrate at surface) |
| Weed pressure | Low (dry inter-rows) | High |
| Labor requirement | Low | High |
| Fertigation capability | Yes | No |
| Initial cost (per hectare) | 8,000–15,000 SAR | 1,000–3,000 SAR |
| Root rot risk | Low | High (waterlogging) |
| Recommended emitters | 4–8 L/h, 2–4 emitters per tree (young); 8–12 emitters (mature) | — |

**Drip irrigation schedule (mature tree, lowland summer):**
- Frequency: Daily in peak summer, every 2–3 days in winter
- Duration: 2–4 hours per session depending on emitter flow rate
- Volume: 80–120 L/tree/day in peak summer; 20–40 L/tree/day in winter
- Reduce irrigation 4–6 weeks before harvest to concentrate sugars

**Water quality thresholds for citrus:**

| Parameter | Ideal | Acceptable | Problematic | Critical |
|-----------|-------|------------|-------------|----------|
| EC (dS/m) | <1.0 | 1.0–2.0 | 2.0–3.0 | >3.0 |
| Chloride (meq/L) | <3 | 3–10 | 10–20 | >20 |
| Sodium (meq/L) | <5 | 5–10 | 10–20 | >20 |
| Boron (mg/L) | <0.5 | 0.5–1.0 | 1.0–2.0 | >2.0 |
| pH | 6.5–7.5 | 7.5–8.0 | >8.0 | >8.5 |
| SAR | <3 | 3–6 | 6–9 | >9 |

> **Warning:** Many wells in coastal Lahj, Abyan, and Jazan have rising salinity (EC 2–5 dS/m) due to over-extraction and seawater intrusion. Blending with rainwater harvesting or switching to salt-tolerant rootstocks (Volkamer lemon, Rangpur lime) is essential in these areas.

### Q5: What is the recommended fertilization program for citrus trees at different growth stages?

**EN:**

Citrus trees have high nutrient demands, particularly for nitrogen (N), potassium (K), and micronutrients like zinc (Zn), iron (Fe), and manganese (Mn) — the latter being commonly deficient in the alkaline soils of the region (pH 7.5–8.5).

**Annual fertilization program for mature bearing trees (per tree/year):**

| Growth Stage | Month(s) | N (g/tree) | P₂O₅ (g/tree) | K₂O (g/tree) | Application Method |
|-------------|----------|-----------|--------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Pre-bloom | Jan–Feb | 150–200 | 100–150 | 50 | Soil (fertigation or band) |
| Flowering & fruit set | Mar–Apr | 100–150 | 50 | 100–150 | Fertigation preferred |
| Fruit development | May–Jul | 200–250 | 50 | 200–250 | Split into 3 monthly doses via fertigation |
| Fruit maturation | Aug–Oct | 50 | 0 | 150–200 | Reduce N, increase K for sugar & color |
| Post-harvest | Nov–Dec | 100 | 100 | 50 | Soil application + organic matter |
| **Annual Total** | — | **600–750** | **300–350** | **550–700** | — |

**Young non-bearing trees (annual rates):**

| Tree Age | N (g/tree/year) | P₂O₅ (g/tree/year) | K₂O (g/tree/year) |
|----------|----------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Year 1 | 50–80 | 30–50 | 30–40 |
| Year 2 | 100–150 | 50–80 | 60–80 |
| Year 3 | 200–300 | 80–120 | 120–160 |
| Year 4+ (bearing) | 600–750 | 300–350 | 550–700 |

**Micronutrient program (foliar sprays, 2–3 times/year):**

| Nutrient | Concentration | Timing | Deficiency Symptom |
|----------|--------------|--------|-------------------|
| Zinc (ZnSO₄) | 3–5 g/L | Spring flush, post-harvest | Interveinal chlorosis on young leaves, small leaves |
| Iron (Fe-EDDHA chelate) | 2–3 g/L | Spring, summer | Interveinal chlorosis, green veins on yellow leaves |
| Manganese (MnSO₄) | 2–3 g/L | Spring flush | Interveinal chlorosis similar to Fe but on older leaves |
| Boron (H₃BO₃) | 1–2 g/L | Pre-bloom | Poor fruit set, hard dry fruit, gum pockets |
| Magnesium (MgSO₄) | 10–15 g/L | Summer | Bronze/yellow inverted-V on old leaves |

**Organic matter:** Apply 30–50 kg well-decomposed farmyard manure or compost per tree annually (November–December), spread under the canopy and incorporated lightly.

**Key regional considerations:**
- The alkaline calcareous soils of Hadhramaut and Najran lock up Fe, Zn, and Mn — use chelated forms (EDDHA for Fe) rather than sulfate
- Avoid ammonium-based fertilizers near harvest as they can delay color development
- In saline areas (EC > 2 dS/m), use nitrate-based N sources (calcium nitrate, potassium nitrate) rather than ammonium sulfate to avoid soil acidification stress

### Q6: What are the major diseases affecting citrus in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, and how are they managed?

**EN:**

The warm, humid conditions in lowland zones and the widespread use of susceptible rootstocks (sour orange) make citrus orchards vulnerable to several devastating diseases.

| Disease | Arabic Name | Pathogen | Affected Parts | Severity | Key Regions |
|---------|------------|----------|---------------|----------|-------------|
| Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV) | فيروس تريستيزا الحمضيات | *Closterovirus* (aphid-vectored) | Whole tree (decline, death) | Very High | Lahj, Abyan, Jazan (sour orange rootstock) |
| Gummosis / Foot Rot | التصمغ / تعفن القدم | *Phytophthora citrophthora*, *P. nicotianae* | Trunk base, crown roots | High | All irrigated zones, especially flood-irrigated |
| Root Rot | تعفن الجذور | *Phytophthora* spp., *Fusarium* | Root system | High | Waterlogged areas, heavy clay soils |
| Citrus Canker | تقرح الحمضيات | *Xanthomonas citri* | Leaves, fruit, twigs | Medium | Jazan (humid), Lahj |
| Sooty Mold | العفن الأسود (الهبابي) | *Capnodium* spp. | Leaf surface (on honeydew) | Low–Medium | All zones (follows scale/whitefly infestations) |
| Citrus Scab | جرب الحمضيات | *Elsinoe fawcettii* | Leaves, fruit rind | Medium | Humid lowlands |
| Mal Secco | مال سيكو (الجفاف) | *Phoma tracheiphila* | Branches, trunk | Medium | Lemon trees in Asir, Hadhramaut |
| Huanglongbing (HLB/Greening) | تخضير الحمضيات | *Candidatus Liberibacter* (psyllid-vectored) | Whole tree | Very High (if introduced) | Not yet confirmed, high risk |

**Detailed management protocols for the 3 most critical diseases:**

**1. Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV):**
- **Prevention (critical):** Replace sour orange rootstock with CTV-tolerant rootstocks (Citrumelo Swingle, Volkamer lemon, Troyer citrange)
- **Vector control:** Manage brown citrus aphid (*Toxoptera citricida*) with imidacloprid (soil drench 1.5 g a.i./tree) or thiamethoxam
- **Nursery certification:** Only use budwood from CTV-tested mother trees; establish certified nurseries
- **Monitoring:** ELISA or RT-PCR testing of new plantings; survey every 2 years
- **Eradication:** Remove and burn infected trees on sour orange rootstock immediately; replant with tolerant rootstock after 6 months

**2. Gummosis (التصمغ) — *Phytophthora*:**
- **Cultural:** Keep bud union above soil level; avoid soil/water contact with trunk; improve drainage; avoid flood irrigation
- **Preventive:** Paint trunk base (ground level to 50 cm height) with Bordeaux paste or copper hydroxide paint annually before rains
- **Curative:** Scrape away diseased bark to healthy tissue; apply metalaxyl-M paste (Ridomil Gold) to the wound; allow to dry
- **Systemic treatment:** Soil drench with metalaxyl-M (2.5 g a.i./m² around trunk) or phosphorous acid injection (20% solution, 10 mL/tree)
- **Drainage:** Install drainage tiles in waterlogged orchards; raise tree planting on mounds (30 cm above grade) in flood-prone areas

**3. Root Rot (*Phytophthora* / *Fusarium*):**
- **Prevention:** Use resistant rootstocks (Trifoliata, Citrumelo); convert from flood to drip irrigation; ensure field drainage
- **Treatment:** Metalaxyl-M soil drench (3 applications: spring, mid-summer, fall); potassium phosphonate foliar spray (3 g/L) every 6 weeks during wet periods
- **Biological control:** Apply *Trichoderma harzianum* (10⁸ CFU/mL, 2 L/tree) to root zone twice yearly
- **Soil management:** Avoid compaction around trees; maintain organic mulch; test soil pH and adjust if needed

### Q7: What are the major pests of citrus and their integrated management strategies?

**EN:**

Citrus orchards in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia face persistent pest pressure due to year-round warm temperatures. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) is essential, combining biological control, cultural practices, and judicious chemical use.

| Pest | Arabic Name | Damage | Severity | Peak Season | Key Regions |
|------|------------|--------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Citrus Leaf Miner (*Phyllocnistis citrella*) | حافرة أوراق الحمضيات (صانعة الأنفاق) | Serpentine mines on young leaves, leaf curling, entry for canker | High | Spring & summer flushes (Mar–Sep) | All regions |
| Mediterranean Fruit Fly (*Ceratitis capitata*) | ذبابة الفاكهة (ذبابة البحر المتوسط) | Oviposition in fruit, larval tunneling, fruit drop and rot | Very High | Fruit maturation (Sep–Mar) | All regions, worst in lowlands |
| Citrus Aphids (*Toxoptera citricida*, *Aphis gossypii*) | مَنّ الحمضيات | Leaf curling, honeydew, sooty mold, CTV virus transmission | High | Spring flush, cool season (Oct–Apr) | All regions |
| Soft Scale (*Coccus hesperidum*) | الحشرة القشرية الرخوة | Sap feeding, honeydew, sooty mold, tree weakening | Medium | Year-round (peaks in warm humid months) | Lowlands |
| California Red Scale (*Aonidiella aurantii*) | الحشرة القشرية الحمراء | Feeding on fruit, leaves, bark; cosmetic damage to fruit | Medium–High | Year-round | All regions |
| Citrus Whitefly (*Dialeurodes citri*) | الذبابة البيضاء | Honeydew, sooty mold, leaf yellowing | Medium | Spring–summer | Humid lowlands |
| Citrus Red Mite (*Panonychus citri*) | الأكاروس الأحمر | Leaf stippling, bronzing, premature leaf drop | Medium | Hot dry months (May–Aug) | Mid-elevation, highlands |
| Citrus Psyllid (*Diaphorina citri*) | سيليد الحمضيات | Direct feeding damage; vector of HLB (greening disease) | Critical (if present) | Year-round | Monitoring needed — not yet established |

**Integrated management protocols:**

**Citrus Leaf Miner:**
- **Biological:** Release *Ageniaspis citricola* parasitoid (where available); conserve native parasitoids by avoiding broad-spectrum sprays during non-critical periods
- **Cultural:** Manage flush timing; prune to synchronize new growth; irrigate/fertilize to avoid continuous flushing
- **Chemical (threshold: >50% of new shoots infested):** Abamectin 1.8% EC (0.5 mL/L) or spinosad (0.3 mL/L) targeting new flush only; rotate active ingredients
- **Timing:** Spray when new leaves are 1–2 cm long (before mines form); do NOT spray mature leaves

**Mediterranean Fruit Fly:**
- **Monitoring:** Deploy McPhail traps or Jackson traps with trimedlure; 1 trap per hectare; check weekly; action threshold: 0.5 flies/trap/day
- **Cultural:** Collect and destroy fallen fruit daily; do not leave unharvested fruit on trees
- **Mass trapping:** Protein bait stations (GF-120 or local hydrolyzed protein bait) at 20–30 stations/ha
- **Bait spray:** Protein hydrolysate + malathion (2 mL/L) or spinosad bait (GF-120) applied to 1 m² patches on tree trunk/canopy, NOT broadcast; repeat weekly during risk period
- **Sterile Insect Technique (SIT):** Where available from government programs (Saudi Arabia has SIT releases in Jazan)
- **Post-harvest:** Cold treatment (1°C for 16 days) for export fruit

**Citrus Aphids (مَنّ):**
- **Biological (primary approach):** Conserve ladybird beetles (*Coccinella*, *Hippodamia*), lacewings (*Chrysoperla*), and syrphid fly larvae; release *Aphidius colemani* parasitoid where available
- **Cultural:** Manage nitrogen to reduce excessive soft growth; ant control (ants protect aphids from predators) using sticky bands around trunks
- **Chemical (threshold: >25% of shoots infested with colonies):** Imidacloprid soil drench (0.5 g a.i./tree for young trees, 1.5 g for mature); or acetamiprid foliar spray (0.25 g/L); or mineral oil 1.5% (suffocating, less disruptive to beneficials)
- **CTV risk:** In areas with CTV, aggressive aphid control on sour orange rootstock is critical during spring flush

**Scale Insects (soft and armored):**

- **Chemical:** Petroleum spray oil 2% (summer oil) during crawler emergence; spirotetramat (Movento) for systemic control; avoid organophosphates that kill beneficials

**بروتوكولات الإدارة المتكاملة:**

**حافرة أوراق الحمضيات:**
- **حيوية:** إطلاق الطفيل *Ageniaspis citricola* (حيث يتوفر)؛ الحفاظ على الطفيليات الطبيعية بتجنب الرش واسع الطيف خلال الفترات غير الحرجة
- **زراعية:** إدارة توقيت النموات؛ التقليم لتوحيد النمو الجديد؛ تنظيم الري والتسميد لتجنب النمو المستمر
- **كيميائية (عتبة: >50% من الأغصان الجديدة مصابة):** أبامكتين 1.8% EC (0.5 مل/لتر) أو سبينوساد (0.3 مل/لتر) على النموات الجديدة فقط؛ تناوب المواد الفعالة
- **التوقيت:** الرش عندما تكون الأوراق بطول 1–2 سم (قبل تكوّن الأنفاق)؛ لا ترش الأوراق الناضجة

**ذبابة الفاكهة:**
- **المراقبة:** نشر مصائد ماكفيل أو جاكسون مع ترايميدلور؛ مصيدة واحدة/هكتار؛ فحص أسبوعي؛ عتبة العمل: 0.5 ذبابة/مصيدة/يوم
- **زراعية:** جمع وإتلاف الثمار المتساقطة يومياً؛ عدم ترك ثمار غير محصودة على الأشجار
- **صيد جماعي:** محطات طُعم بروتيني (GF-120 أو طُعم بروتين محلل محلياً) بمعدل 20–30 محطة/هكتار
- **رش طُعمي:** بروتين محلل + مالاثيون (2 مل/لتر) أو طُعم سبينوساد (GF-120) يُوضع على بقعة 1 م² على الجذع/المظلة، وليس رشاً شاملاً؛ يُكرر أسبوعياً خلال فترة الخطر
- **تقنية الحشرة العقيمة (SIT):** حيث تتوفر من البرامج الحكومية (السعودية لديها إطلاقات SIT في جازان)
- **بعد الحصاد:** معاملة بالبرودة (1°م لمدة 16 يوماً) لثمار التصدير

**مَنّ الحمضيات:**
- **حيوية (النهج الأساسي):** الحفاظ على الخنافس المفترسة (أبو العيد)، أسد المَنّ، يرقات ذبابة السيرفيد؛ إطلاق الطفيل *Aphidius colemani* حيث يتوفر
- **زراعية:** إدارة النيتروجين لتقليل النمو الغض المفرط؛ مكافحة النمل (الذي يحمي المَنّ من المفترسات) بأشرطة لاصقة حول الجذوع
- **كيميائية (عتبة: >25% من الأغصان بها مستعمرات):** إيميداكلوبريد غمر تربة (0.5 جم مادة فعالة/شجرة للصغيرة، 1.5 جم للبالغة)؛ أو أسيتاميبريد رشاً ورقياً (0.25 جم/لتر)؛ أو زيت معدني 1.5% (خانق، أقل ضرراً بالنافعات)
- **خطر CTV:** في مناطق وجود التريستيزا، المكافحة المكثفة للمَنّ على أصل النارنج حرجة خلال النموة الربيعية

**الحشرات القشرية (الرخوة والمدرعة):**
- **حيوية:** إطلاق *Cryptolaemus montrouzieri* أو طفيليات *Metaphycus*؛ الحفاظ على الأعداء الطبيعية
- **زراعية:** التقليم لفتح المظلة (يحسن التهوية، يعرض القشريات للحرارة والمفترسات)؛ مكافحة النمل
- **كيميائية:** زيت بترولي 2% (زيت صيفي) أثناء خروج الزاحفات؛ سبيروتيترامات (موفنتو) للمكافحة الجهازية؛ تجنب الفوسفورية العضوية التي تقتل النافعات

**Management practices for saline conditions:**
1. **Leaching fraction:** Apply 15–20% extra irrigation water beyond crop ET to leach salts below root zone
2. **Drip irrigation:** Maintains a continuous low-salinity zone near emitters where roots concentrate
3. **Mulching:** Reduces evaporation and salt accumulation at soil surface
4. **Gypsum application:** 2–5 tons/ha for sodic soils (SAR > 6) to displace sodium
5. **Foliar nutrition:** Bypass root uptake problems by foliar-applying Zn, Fe, Mn, and K

**Post-harvest handling recommendations:**
- **Harvest method:** Hand-pick with clippers leaving 2–3 mm stem; never pull fruit
- **Harvest time:** Early morning (before 10 AM) when fruit temperature is lowest
- **Field sorting:** Remove damaged, sunburned, or pest-infested fruit immediately

**توصيات ما بعد الحصاد:**
- **طريقة الحصاد:** القطف اليدوي بالمقص مع ترك 2–3 مم من العنق؛ لا تسحب الثمار أبداً
- **وقت الحصاد:** الصباح الباكر (قبل 10 صباحاً) عندما تكون حرارة الثمار أقل ما يمكن
- **الفرز الحقلي:** إزالة الثمار المتضررة أو المحروقة بالشمس أو المصابة بالآفات فوراً
- **الغسيل:** ماء نظيف + غمس في هيبوكلوريت الصوديوم (200 جزء بالمليون) لمدة دقيقتين
- **التشميع:** طبقة شمع غذائي تقلل فقد الوزن بنسبة 30–40% أثناء التخزين
- **التخزين:** البرتقال الحلو على 5–8°م، رطوبة 85–90% لمدة 8–12 أسبوع؛ الليمون على 10–13°م لمدة 4–8 أسابيع؛ الليمون الحامض على 9–10°م لمدة 6–8 أسابيع
- **تجنب أضرار البرودة:** لا تخزن الليمون الحامض تحت 8°م أو البرتقال تحت 3°م
