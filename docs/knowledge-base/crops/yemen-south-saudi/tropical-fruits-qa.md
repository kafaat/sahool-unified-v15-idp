# Tropical Fruits Q&A: Yemen & Southern Saudi Arabia | أسئلة وأجوبة الفواكه الاستوائية: اليمن وجنوب السعودية

### Q1: What are the main mango varieties cultivated in Yemen and the Jazan region of Saudi Arabia, and how do they differ?

**EN:**
Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia grow a diverse range of mango varieties, each suited to local microclimates and market preferences. The primary growing areas are the Tihama coastal plain (Yemen), Lahj and Abyan governorates, and the Jazan region of Saudi Arabia.

| Variety | Origin | Fruit Size | Flavor Profile | Region | Season |
|---------|--------|-----------|----------------|--------|--------|
| Taimour (تيمور) | Egyptian/Indian | Medium-Large (350–500 g) | Sweet, aromatic, low fiber | Tihama, Lahj | June–August |
| Hindi (هندي) | Indian | Small-Medium (200–350 g) | Very sweet, high fiber | Tihama, Jazan | May–July |
| Zibdiya (زبدية) | Local Yemeni | Medium (300–450 g) | Buttery, creamy texture, low fiber | Lahj, Abyan | June–August |
| Alphonso (ألفونسو) | Indian | Medium (250–350 g) | Intensely sweet, aromatic | Jazan (trial) | June–July |
| Keitt (كيت) | American | Large (500–800 g) | Mild, juicy, late season | Jazan | August–September |
| Tommy Atkins (تومي أتكنز) | American | Large (450–650 g) | Firm, good shelf life | Jazan | July–August |
| Sudani (سوداني) | Sudanese | Small (150–250 g) | Sweet, fibrous, hardy | Tihama lowlands | May–July |
| Baladi (بلدي) | Local seedling | Variable (200–400 g) | Variable, often fibrous | All regions | June–August |

Taimour dominates commercial orchards in Lahj due to its premium price and export potential. Hindi is the most widely planted variety in Tihama due to heat tolerance and productivity. Zibdiya is prized for its buttery flesh and commands premium local market prices. In Jazan, government-supported programs have introduced Keitt and Tommy Atkins for extended season coverage and export viability.

### Q2: What are the climate and geographic requirements for tropical fruit cultivation in these regions, and which areas are most suitable?

**EN:**
Tropical fruit cultivation in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia is concentrated in lowland coastal and semi-coastal zones where temperatures and humidity support mango, banana, papaya, and guava production. The key requirement is freedom from frost combined with adequate heat accumulation.

| Parameter | Mango | Banana | Papaya | Guava |
|-----------|-------|--------|--------|-------|
| Optimal Temp (°C) | 24–30 | 26–30 | 25–32 | 23–28 |
| Min Temp Tolerance (°C) | 10 (young trees die below 5) | 15 (growth stops below 12) | 12 (dies below 5) | 5 (tolerates brief frost) |
| Max Temp Tolerance (°C) | 45 (with irrigation) | 38 (leaf scorch above 40) | 40 | 42 |
| Annual Rainfall (mm) | 500–2,500 | 1,200–2,500 | 1,000–2,000 | 600–1,500 |
| Relative Humidity (%) | 50–80 | 60–90 | 60–85 | 50–70 |
| Altitude (m asl) | 0–600 | 0–300 | 0–400 | 0–1,200 |
| Dry Season Required? | Yes (2–3 months for flowering) | No | No | Yes (improves fruiting) |

**Growing Regions:**

| Region | Country | Altitude (m) | Avg Temp (°C) | Annual Rain (mm) | Main Crops | Limitations |
|--------|---------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|------------|-------------|
| Tihama Plain | Yemen | 0–200 | 28–35 | 50–150 | Mango, Banana, Papaya | Extreme heat, water scarcity, salinity |
| Lahj (Delta) | Yemen | 0–300 | 27–33 | 50–100 | Mango, Guava | Limited water, pest pressure |
| Abyan Coast | Yemen | 0–150 | 28–34 | 50–120 | Mango, Banana | Salinity, infrastructure |
| Jazan Coastal | Saudi Arabia | 0–200 | 27–38 | 100–300 | Mango, Banana, Papaya, Guava | High humidity, pest pressure |
| Jazan Foothills | Saudi Arabia | 200–600 | 22–32 | 200–400 | Mango, Guava | Limited flat land |

Mango requires a distinct dry season of 2–3 months before flowering to trigger bloom. The Tihama plain and Lahj provide this naturally. Banana requires continuous moisture and cannot tolerate a prolonged dry spell without supplemental irrigation. Papaya is highly sensitive to waterlogging and cold, making well-drained sandy loams in coastal areas ideal. Guava is the most tolerant of all four crops and can succeed even in the Jazan foothills at moderate elevations.

### Q3: What are the recommended planting and propagation methods for mango and other tropical fruits in the region?

**EN:**
Vegetative propagation, particularly grafting, is essential for commercial tropical fruit production in Yemen and Jazan. Seedling trees are unreliable in variety characteristics, take 5–7 years to bear fruit, and often produce inferior quality. Grafted trees bear fruit in 3–4 years with known varietal traits.

**Mango Propagation Methods:**

| Method | Success Rate | Best Period | Rootstock | Scion Age | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------------|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Veneer grafting (التطعيم القشري) | 70–85% | Feb–April | Seedling 12–18 months | Current season wood | Preferred method in Yemen |
| Cleft grafting (التطعيم بالشق) | 60–75% | March–April | Seedling 12–18 months | Dormant wood | Common in Jazan nurseries |
| Side grafting (التطعيم الجانبي) | 65–80% | Feb–May | Seedling 8–12 months | Semi-hardwood | Good for small rootstock |
| Inarching (التطعيم بالتقريب) | 85–95% | Any time | Adjacent seedling | Mother tree branch | Traditional, labor-intensive |
| Air layering (الترقيد الهوائي) | 50–70% | July–September | N/A | Mature branch | Weak root system, not recommended |

**Planting Guidelines by Crop:**

| Parameter | Mango | Banana | Papaya | Guava |
|-----------|-------|--------|--------|-------|
| Spacing | 8×8 m (traditional), 5×5 m (high-density) | 2.5×2.5 m | 2×2 m or 2.5×2.5 m | 5×5 m or 6×6 m |
| Trees/ha | 156 (traditional), 400 (high-density) | 1,600 | 2,500 (at 2×2 m) | 278–400 |
| Pit size (cm) | 100×100×100 | 60×60×60 | 50×50×50 | 60×60×60 |
| Pit preparation | Fill with topsoil + 20 kg manure + 500 g SSP | 10 kg manure + 200 g SSP | 5 kg manure + 100 g SSP | 10 kg manure + 300 g SSP |
| Best planting time | July–August (monsoon onset) | Year-round (with irrigation) | Sept–Oct or Feb–March | July–August |
| Propagation | Grafted seedling | Suckers or tissue culture | Seed (direct or nursery) | Grafted or air-layered |
| Years to first fruit | 3–4 (grafted), 5–7 (seedling) | 12–14 months | 8–10 months | 2–3 (grafted) |

**Key Recommendations:**
- Use polyethylene shade structures (50% shade) for new mango transplants during the first summer in Tihama, where temperatures regularly exceed 42°C
- For banana, tissue-culture plantlets are preferred over traditional suckers to ensure disease-free planting material, though suckers remain common due to cost
- Papaya should be planted with 3 seedlings per pit, thinning to one female or hermaphrodite plant after flowering
- Windbreaks (Conocarpus or date palm) are critical on exposed Tihama sites to protect banana and papaya from hot desiccating winds

### Q4: How should irrigation be managed for tropical fruit trees in the hot arid conditions of Tihama, Lahj, and Jazan?

**EN:**
Water management is the single most critical factor for tropical fruit success in these regions, where annual rainfall is typically 50–300 mm but crop water demand ranges from 1,200–2,500 mm. Efficient irrigation systems and precise scheduling are essential.

**Crop Water Requirements (Annual):**

| Crop | Young Trees (L/tree/day) | Mature Trees (L/tree/day) | Peak Demand (L/tree/day) | Annual Requirement (mm) | Critical Stress Periods |
|------|-------------------------|--------------------------|-------------------------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Mango | 20–40 | 80–150 | 200–300 (fruiting) | 1,200–1,800 | Flowering, fruit set, fruit development |
| Banana | 15–25 (per plant) | 30–50 (per plant) | 60–80 (bunch filling) | 1,800–2,500 | Continuous; no drought tolerance |
| Papaya | 10–20 | 25–40 | 50–60 (fruiting) | 1,500–2,000 | Flowering, fruit fill |
| Guava | 15–30 | 50–100 | 150–200 (fruiting) | 1,000–1,500 | Fruit development, pre-harvest |

**Irrigation Systems Comparison:**

| System | Water Efficiency | Installation Cost (USD/ha) | Suitability | Common In |
|--------|-----------------|---------------------------|-------------|-----------|
| Flood (traditional) | 30–40% | 200–500 | Not recommended | Declining in all areas |
| Basin irrigation | 40–55% | 500–1,000 | Mango only (mature) | Lahj traditional orchards |
| Drip (surface) | 85–92% | 2,500–4,000 | All crops | Jazan modern farms |
| Drip (subsurface) | 90–95% | 3,500–5,500 | Banana, papaya | Jazan government projects |
| Micro-sprinkler | 75–85% | 2,000–3,500 | Mango, guava (mature) | Tihama progressive farms |
| Bubbler | 80–88% | 2,200–3,800 | Mango, guava | Limited adoption |

**Seasonal Irrigation Schedule for Mango (Mature Tree, Drip System):**

| Month | Stage | Irrigation Interval | Volume (L/tree/day) | EC Max (dS/m) | Notes |
|-------|-------|--------------------|--------------------|---------------|-------|
| Oct–Nov | Post-harvest recovery | Every 5–7 days | 40–60 | 2.0 | Reduce gradually to induce dormancy |
| Dec–Jan | Dormancy/Stress | Every 10–15 days or withhold | 0–20 | N/A | Dry stress triggers flowering |
| Feb–March | Flowering | Every 3–4 days | 60–80 | 1.5 | Avoid overhead wetting (disease risk) |
| April–May | Fruit set and development | Every 2–3 days | 100–150 | 1.5 | Critical period; no water stress |
| June–July | Fruit maturation | Every 2 days | 150–200 | 1.5 | Reduce 2 weeks before harvest for sugar |
| Aug–Sept | Vegetative flush | Every 3–4 days | 80–120 | 2.0 | Support new growth |

**Water Quality Concerns:**
- Groundwater salinity in Tihama often exceeds 2.0–4.0 dS/m, above the tolerance threshold for banana (1.0 dS/m) and papaya (1.5 dS/m)
- Mango tolerates up to 2.5 dS/m and guava up to 4.0 dS/m, making them better suited to saline conditions
- Blending well water with harvested rainwater or wadi flood water improves quality
- Mulching with 10–15 cm of organic material reduces evaporation by 30–40% and is strongly recommended

### Q5: What fertilization programs are recommended for mango and banana in these tropical fruit-growing areas?

**EN:**
Fertilization programs must account for the typically sandy, low-organic-matter soils of Tihama and coastal Jazan, where nutrient leaching is rapid and organic matter content often falls below 0.5%. Soil testing before each season is essential, as blanket recommendations can lead to imbalanced nutrition.

**Mango Fertilization Program (per tree/year):**

| Tree Age | Organic Manure (kg) | N (g) | P2O5 (g) | K2O (g) | Micronutrients | Application Timing |
|----------|---------------------|-------|----------|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| 1–2 years | 10–15 | 100–150 | 50–75 | 50–75 | Foliar Fe+Zn spray 2× | 3 split doses: Feb, May, Aug |
| 3–4 years | 20–25 | 200–300 | 100–150 | 150–200 | Foliar Fe+Zn+Mn spray 3× | 3 split doses: Feb, May, Aug |
| 5–7 years | 30–40 | 400–600 | 200–300 | 300–400 | Foliar + soil Zn sulfate 50 g | 4 splits: Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug |
| 8–15 years | 40–50 | 750–1,000 | 300–400 | 500–750 | Full micro package 4× | 4 splits: Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug |
| 15+ years | 50–75 | 1,000–1,500 | 400–500 | 750–1,000 | Full micro package 4× | 4 splits: Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug |

**Critical Mango Nutrition Notes:**
- Reduce nitrogen 2 months before expected flowering (Nov–Dec) to avoid excessive vegetative growth that suppresses bloom
- Potassium is critical during fruit development (April–June); apply potassium sulfate (K2SO4) rather than muriate of potash (KCl) in saline soils
- Zinc deficiency is extremely common in calcareous Tihama soils; foliar spray of ZnSO4 (0.5%) + urea (1%) at new flush is essential
- Boron deficiency causes internal fruit breakdown; apply borax at 50–100 g/tree if soil B < 0.5 ppm

| **Total/cycle** | — | **300** | **125** | **525** | **15** | N:P:K ratio approximately 2:1:4 |

**Key Banana Nutrition Points:**
- Banana is a potassium-demanding crop; the N:K ratio should be approximately 1:1.75 to 1:2
- In sandy Tihama soils, apply fertilizer in 8–10 split doses through fertigation rather than 3–4 large soil applications
- Magnesium deficiency (yellowing of older leaves with green veins) is common; apply MgSO4 at 100–150 g/plant/year

### Q6: What are the major pests affecting tropical fruits in Yemen and Jazan, and how should they be managed?

**EN:**
Tropical fruit production in Tihama, Lahj, Abyan, and Jazan faces significant pest pressure year-round due to the warm climate. Integrated Pest Management (IPM) combining cultural, biological, and targeted chemical methods is essential.

**Major Pests by Crop:**

| Pest | Scientific Name | Crops Affected | Damage | Severity | Season |
|------|----------------|----------------|--------|----------|--------|
| Fruit fly (ذبابة الفاكهة) | *Bactrocera zonata*, *B. dorsalis* | Mango, guava, papaya | Oviposition in fruit, larval feeding, fruit rot | Critical | April–September |
| Mango hopper (نطاط المانجو) | *Idioscopus clypealis*, *I. niveosparsus* | Mango | Sucks sap from panicles, secretes honeydew, flower drop | High | February–April (flowering) |
| Mango stem borer (حفار ساق المانجو) | *Batocera rufomaculata* | Mango | Larval tunneling in trunk and branches, tree death | High | Year-round |
| Mango seed weevil (سوسة بذرة المانجو) | *Sternochetus mangiferae* | Mango | Larva feeds inside seed, reduces germination | Medium | May–July (fruiting) |
| Mango mealybug (بق المانجو الدقيقي) | *Drosicha mangiferae* | Mango | Sucks sap from shoots, flowers, fruits | Medium | Dec–March |
| Banana weevil (سوسة الموز) | *Cosmopolites sordidus* | Banana | Larval boring in corm, plant toppling | High | Year-round |
| Banana aphid (من الموز) | *Pentalonia nigronervosa* | Banana | Vector of Banana Bunchy Top Virus (BBTV) | Critical (as vector) | Year-round |
| Banana thrips (تربس الموز) | *Thrips hawaiiensis* | Banana | Scarring of fruit skin, cosmetic damage | Medium | Year-round |
| Papaya mealybug (بق البابايا الدقيقي) | *Paracoccus marginatus* | Papaya, guava | Heavy infestations defoliate and kill young trees | High | Year-round |
| Guava fruit fly (ذبابة ثمار الجوافة) | *Bactrocera correcta* | Guava | Larval feeding in fruit pulp | High | Fruiting season |
| Red spider mite (العنكبوت الأحمر) | *Oligonychus mangiferus* | Mango, guava | Leaf stippling, defoliation in dry conditions | Medium | Mar–June (hot, dry) |
| Scale insects (الحشرات القشرية) | Multiple species | All tropical fruits | Sap sucking, sooty mold, weakened trees | Medium | Year-round |

**IPM Strategies for Key Pests:**

**1. Fruit Fly Management (Priority 1):**

| Method | Description | Timing | Effectiveness |
|--------|-------------|--------|---------------|
| Methyl eugenol traps | Male annihilation technique; 10–15 traps/ha with insecticide block | 6 weeks before harvest, monitor year-round | High (50–70% population reduction) |
| Protein bait sprays | GF-120 or yeast + malathion bait applied to 1 m² spot per tree | Weekly from fruit set to harvest | High (60–80% reduction) |
| Sanitation | Collect and destroy fallen/infested fruit daily; bury >50 cm deep | Throughout fruiting | Essential (removes breeding sites) |
| Bagging | Individual fruit bags (paper or non-woven fabric) at marble stage | 30–40 days after fruit set | Very high (>90% protection) |
| Biological control | *Fopius arisanus* (parasitoid); *Metarhizium anisopliae* soil drench | Ongoing; soil treatment pre-fruiting | Moderate (supplementary) |
| Sterile insect technique (SIT) | Area-wide release of sterile males | Government programs only | High (area-wide) |

**2. Mango Hopper Management:**

| Method | Description | Timing |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Neem oil spray (2–3%) | Repels and disrupts feeding; safe during flowering | Pre-bloom and early bloom |
| Imidacloprid 17.8% SL | Systemic; 0.3 ml/L soil drench or foliar spray | 2 weeks before flowering |
| Thiamethoxam 25% WG | Foliar spray 0.3 g/L | At first appearance of hoppers |
| Cultural: Orchard sanitation | Remove weed hosts, prune dense canopy for air circulation | Post-harvest |
| Light traps | Attract and monitor hopper populations | Flowering season |

**3. Banana Weevil Management:**

| Method | Description | Timing |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| Clean planting material | Use tissue culture or pared and hot-water treated suckers (52°C, 20 min) | At planting |
| Pseudostem traps | Split pseudostem sections placed around plants; collect and destroy weevils | Year-round, check 2×/week |
| *Beauveria bassiana* | Biological control fungus applied to cut pseudostems | Year-round |
| Fipronil or chlorpyrifos | Corm treatment at planting; spot treatment if infestation severe | At planting, or when detected |

### Q7: What are the major diseases of tropical fruits in these regions, and what control measures are effective?

**EN:**
Diseases pose significant challenges to tropical fruit quality and yield in Yemen and Jazan. The combination of high humidity during certain seasons, warm temperatures year-round, and limited access to modern fungicides makes disease management particularly difficult.

**Major Diseases by Crop:**

| Disease | Pathogen | Crops | Symptoms | Severity | Favorable Conditions |
|---------|----------|-------|----------|----------|---------------------|
| Anthracnose (أنثراكنوز) | *Colletotrichum gloeosporioides* | Mango, papaya, guava | Black sunken lesions on fruit, blossom blight, leaf spots | Critical | High humidity >80%, rain during flowering |
| Powdery mildew (البياض الدقيقي) | *Oidium mangiferae* | Mango | White powdery coating on panicles, young leaves, and fruit | High | Cool nights (15–20°C), dry days, high RH |
| Dieback/Tip dieback (جفاف الأطراف) | *Lasiodiplodia theobromae* | Mango | Branch dieback from tip downward, gum exudation | High | Stress (water, heat), wounds from pruning |

**Ratoon Management:**
- Select the strongest sucker (follower) at 60–90 cm height when the mother plant flowers
- Remove all other suckers (desuckering) every 4–6 weeks using a sharp, sterilized tool
- After harvest, cut the mother pseudostem to ground level to feed nutrients back to the ratoon
- Ratoon crops mature 2–3 months faster than plant crops but produce smaller bunches (15–20% less)
- Replace the plantation after 3–4 ratoon cycles as yields decline significantly

**Bunch Management:**
- Remove the male bud (bell) when all female hands are set (saves 5–8% bunch weight for fruit development)
- Remove the last 1–2 false hands (with very small fingers) to improve remaining finger size
- Support bunches with bamboo or rope to prevent toppling in wind
- Bag bunches with blue polyethylene sleeves to protect from sunburn, insects, and cosmetic damage; bagged fruit matures 1–2 weeks earlier

**Key Challenges in Yemen:**
- Banana Bunchy Top Virus (BBTV) has been detected in Tihama and is spreading; roguing infected plants and controlling aphid vectors is urgent
- Groundwater depletion and increasing salinity threaten long-term viability in many Tihama sites
- Flood irrigation wastes 50–60% of water; transitioning to drip requires capital investment that smallholders lack
- Cold-chain infrastructure is absent in most areas, causing 25–40% post-harvest losses

### Q10: What are the main post-harvest handling challenges for tropical fruits in Yemen and Jazan, and what solutions are available?

**EN:**
Post-harvest losses for tropical fruits in Yemen are estimated at 30–50%, among the highest rates globally. In Jazan, losses are lower (15–25%) due to better infrastructure but remain significant. The hot climate, fragile fruit nature, poor transport networks, and lack of cold-chain infrastructure are the primary causes.

**Post-Harvest Loss Estimates by Crop and Stage:**

| Loss Stage | Mango | Banana | Papaya | Guava |
|-----------|-------|--------|--------|-------|
| Harvesting damage | 5–10% | 3–5% | 5–8% | 3–5% |
| Handling and sorting | 5–8% | 5–8% | 8–12% | 5–8% |
| Transport | 10–15% | 8–12% | 10–15% | 8–12% |
| Market/retail | 5–10% | 5–10% | 10–15% | 5–10% |
| **Total estimated loss** | **25–43%** | **21–35%** | **33–50%** | **21–35%** |

**Harvest Maturity Indicators:**

| Crop | Visual Indicators | Physical Indicators | Chemical Indicators | Best Harvest Time |
|------|------------------|--------------------|--------------------|-------------------|
| Mango | Shoulder development, color break (green to yellow tinge) | Specific gravity >1.01 (sinks in water), slight give on squeeze | TSS 8–12 °Brix at harvest, acid 1–2% | Early morning (6–9 AM), 100–110 days after fruit set |
| Banana | Angularity of fingers reduces (filling), light green color | Pulp-to-peel ratio >1.2, 75% maturity for transport | Starch-to-sugar ratio high | Early morning, cut whole bunch |
| Papaya | 1–2 yellow streaks on skin (color break stage) | Slight softening at blossom end, latex turns watery | TSS 11–13 °Brix | Color break for distant market, 25% yellow for local |
| Guava | Color changes from dark to light green, aromatic smell | Slight give when pressed, easily detaches from branch | TSS 8–12 °Brix, titratable acidity 0.3–0.5% | Early morning when dew dries, firm-ripe stage |

**Recommended Post-Harvest Treatments:**

| Treatment | Mango | Banana | Papaya | Guava | Purpose |
|-----------|-------|--------|--------|-------|---------|
| Hot water dip | 52°C, 5 min | Not used | 48°C, 20 min | 46°C, 10 min | Anthracnose, fruit fly disinfestation |
| Calcium chloride dip | 2% CaCl2, 5 min | Not standard | 2% CaCl2, 3 min | 2% CaCl2, 5 min | Firmness retention, shelf life |
| Waxing/coating | Carnauba or shellac | Not used | Food-grade wax | Beeswax coating | Moisture loss reduction |
| Ethylene management | Avoid ethylene exposure | Ethylene ripening (100 ppm, 24h) | Ethylene ripening as needed | Avoid ethylene exposure | Controlled ripening |
| Cool storage temp | 10–13°C | 13–14°C (ripe: 12°C) | 7–10°C (ripe), 10–12°C (green) | 8–10°C | Extend shelf life |
| Storage humidity | 85–90% RH | 90–95% RH | 85–90% RH | 85–90% RH | Prevent shriveling |
| Shelf life (ambient 30°C) | 4–7 days | 3–5 days (ripe) | 3–5 days | 2–4 days | Without cold storage |
| Shelf life (cold storage) | 2–3 weeks | 2–3 weeks | 2–3 weeks | 2–3 weeks | With temperature management |

**Market Chain Challenges and Solutions:**

| Challenge | Impact | Current Situation | Recommended Solution | Priority |
|-----------|--------|-------------------|---------------------|----------|
| No cold storage | 30–40% loss in Yemen | Only 2–3 cold stores in all of Tihama | Solar-powered cold rooms (5–10 t capacity) at collection points | Critical |
| Poor road infrastructure | Mechanical damage, delays | 6–12 hour travel from Tihama to Sana'a | Plastic crates instead of wooden boxes; cushioned trucks | High |
| No grading standards | Price suppression, mixed quality | All fruit sold as ungraded bulk | Establish 3-grade system (export, premium, standard) | High |
| Middleman dependency | 30–50% of retail price goes to middlemen | Farmers have no direct market access | Farmer cooperatives with collection centers | High |
| Fruit fly quarantine | Export barriers | Many countries ban imports from fruit fly areas | Area-wide fruit fly management; treatment facilities | Critical |
| Lack of processing facilities | Seasonal glut causes collapse of prices | Minimal processing exists | Juice, pulp, dried fruit facilities; attract private investment | Medium |
| No traceability | Cannot access premium markets | No traceability system | QR-code based farm-to-market tracking | Medium |

| Market | Package Type | Capacity | Cost (USD) | Advantages |
