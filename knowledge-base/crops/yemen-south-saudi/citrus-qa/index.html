    Citrus Q&A: Yemen & Southern Saudi Arabia | أسئلة وأجوبة الحمضيات: اليمن وجنوب السعودية¶

    Q1: What are the main citrus-growing regions in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, and what makes each region suitable?¶

    EN:

    Citrus cultivation in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia spans several distinct agro-ecological zones, each offering particular advantages:

    Region Country Elevation Climate Type Key Advantage Main Citrus Grown
    Lahj (Tuban Delta) Yemen 0–50 m Hot coastal Year-round warmth, alluvial soils Acid lime, sweet orange
    Abyan (Delta Abyan) Yemen 0–80 m Hot coastal Fertile wadi deposits, spate irrigation Acid lime, lemon
    Hadhramaut (Wadi Hadhramaut) Yemen 500–700 m Arid inland valley Deep alluvial aquifers, low humidity Sweet orange, mandarin
    Sana'a Basin Yemen 2,200+ m Temperate highland Cool winters for dormancy, low pest pressure Mandarin, sweet orange
    Tihama Plain Yemen/Saudi 0–50 m Hot humid coastal Long growing season, high heat units Acid lime, grapefruit
    Jazan (جازان) Saudi Arabia 0–400 m Tropical coastal Abundant groundwater, modern drip infrastructure Lime, lemon, orange
    Asir Highlands (عسير) Saudi Arabia 1,500–2,400 m Semi-temperate Cool nights improve fruit quality and color Mandarin, sweet orange
    Al-Baha (الباحة) Saudi Arabia 1,500–2,000 m Semi-temperate Terraced agriculture, adequate rainfall (250–400 mm) Mandarin, lemon

    Lahj and Abyan together account for roughly 60% of Yemen's citrus output, while Jazan province dominates southern Saudi production. Highland areas (Sana'a, Asir, Al-Baha) produce smaller volumes but achieve superior fruit color and sugar-acid balance due to the diurnal temperature range (difference between day and night temperatures often exceeding 15°C).

    EN:

    Sweet Orange Local Baladi Orange برتقال بلدي Lahj, Hadhramaut 10–13 Adapted to local conditions

    Q3: When is the best time to plant citrus trees, and what spacing should be used?¶

    EN:

    Planting Time:

    Region Type Optimal Planting Window Avoid Planting Reason
    Coastal (Lahj, Abyan, Tihama, Jazan) October–February May–August Extreme heat (>42°C) causes transplant shock
    Inland Valley (Hadhramaut) November–January June–September Hot dry winds (>45°C) desiccate young trees
    Highland (Sana'a, Asir, Al-Baha) February–April November–January Frost risk below 0°C damages young grafts

    Container-grown (potted) nursery trees can extend the planting window by 4–6 weeks compared to bare-root stock, provided drip irrigation is available at transplant.

    Spacing Recommendations:

    Citrus Type Spacing (m) Trees/Hectare Canopy at Maturity Notes
    Acid Lime (Baladi) 5 × 5 400 4–5 m diameter Can use 4 × 4 with annual pruning
    Acid Lime (Tahiti) 6 × 5 333 5–6 m More vigorous than Mexican lime
    Lemon (Eureka) 6 × 5 333 5–6 m Semi-dwarf on Citrumelo: 5 × 4
    Sweet Orange 6 × 6 278 6–7 m Standard spacing
    Sweet Orange (High-density) 5 × 3 667 Hedgerow pruned Requires annual mechanical hedging
    Mandarin 5 × 5 400 4–5 m Smaller canopy than orange
    Grapefruit 7 × 7 204 7–8 m Largest canopy, needs space

    Planting procedure: 1. Dig hole 60 × 60 × 60 cm, two weeks before planting 2. Mix excavated soil with 20 kg well-decomposed organic matter + 500 g superphosphate 3. Plant graft union 10–15 cm above soil level (critical to prevent foot rot) 4. Create a basin (60 cm diameter) for irrigation 5. Apply 5–10 cm mulch layer (palm fronds, straw) keeping 15 cm clear of trunk 6. Irrigate immediately after planting (20–30 liters per tree) 7. Install shade cloth (50% shade) for first summer in coastal zones

    Q4: What are the irrigation requirements for citrus in arid and semi-arid conditions, and how does water quality affect production?¶

    EN:

    Citrus trees are sensitive to both water deficit and waterlogging. In the hot arid climate of Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, irrigation management is the single most critical factor determining orchard success.

    Annual Water Requirements by Region:

    Region Annual ET₀ (mm) Citrus Crop Coefficient (Kc) Annual Water Need (mm) Liters/Tree/Year (mature)
    Coastal Tihama/Jazan 2,000–2,400 0.65–0.75 1,300–1,800 18,000–25,000
    Lahj/Abyan 1,800–2,200 0.65–0.75 1,170–1,650 16,000–23,000
    Hadhramaut (Wadi) 2,200–2,600 0.60–0.70 1,320–1,820 18,000–25,000
    Sana'a Highland 1,400–1,700 0.55–0.65 770–1,100 11,000–15,000
    Asir/Al-Baha Highland 1,300–1,600 0.55–0.65 715–1,040 10,000–14,500

    Drip vs. Flood Irrigation Comparison:

    Parameter Drip Irrigation Flood/Basin Irrigation
    Water use efficiency 85–95% 40–55%
    Water saving vs. flood 40–60% less water Baseline
    Salinity management Excellent (continuous leaching) Poor (salt accumulation between irrigations)
    Root rot risk Low (controlled moisture) High (saturated root zone)
    Weed pressure Low (dry inter-rows) High (wet surface)
    Infrastructure cost 3,000–5,000 SAR/ha 500–1,000 SAR/ha
    Labor requirement Low (automated) High (manual)
    Recommended for All regions, especially saline water Highland areas with good water only
    Emitter configuration 4–8 emitters/tree (4 L/h), dual lateral N/A

    Critical Irrigation Periods (do not allow water stress): - Flowering (February–April): Deficit causes flower drop, up to 80% yield loss - Fruit set (April–June): Deficit causes June drop, small fruit size - Fruit expansion (June–September): Deficit reduces juice content and weight - Pre-harvest (2–3 weeks before harvest): Slight deficit can improve sugar concentration

    Water Quality Thresholds for Citrus:

    Parameter Ideal Acceptable Problematic Action Required
    EC (dS/m) < 1.0 1.0–2.0 2.0–3.0 Switch to salt-tolerant rootstock
    SAR < 3 3–6 > 6 Add gypsum, improve drainage
    Chloride (meq/L) < 4 4–10 > 10 Avoid overhead sprinkler, use drip
    Boron (mg/L) < 0.5 0.5–1.0 > 1.0 Blend with low-B water source
    pH 6.5–7.5 7.5–8.0 > 8.5 Acidify irrigation water
    Bicarbonate (meq/L) < 2 2–5 > 5 Acidify to prevent emitter clogging

    In Lahj and Abyan, groundwater salinity has been increasing (now commonly 2.0–4.0 dS/m) due to over-extraction. Using Volkamer Lemon or Cleopatra Mandarin rootstock combined with drip irrigation and a 15–20% leaching fraction is essential for sustained production under these conditions.

    Q5: What is the recommended fertilization program for citrus trees at different growth stages?¶

    EN:

    Detailed Management for Top 3 Diseases:

    1. Citrus Tristeza Virus (CTV) — فيروس تريستيزا الحمضيات: - Vector: Brown Citrus Aphid (Toxoptera citricida) and other aphid species (مَنّ الحمضيات البني) - Symptoms: Quick decline of trees on sour orange rootstock; stem pitting in sweet orange; seedling yellows in grapefruit and lemon - Management: - Replace sour orange rootstock with CTV-tolerant alternatives (Citrumelo Swingle, Volkamer Lemon, Troyer Citrange) - Use certified virus-free budwood from approved nurseries - Control aphid vectors with imidacloprid (soil drench) or lambda-cyhalothrin (foliar) - Remove and destroy infected trees immediately to prevent spread - Cross-protection with mild CTV strains is practiced in some Saudi programs

    2. Phytophthora Root Rot and Crown Rot — تعفن الجذور والتاج: - Conditions: Waterlogged soils, flood irrigation, poor drainage, planting graft union below soil line - Symptoms: Canopy thinning, pale leaves, root discoloration, bark peeling at crown, sudden tree collapse in severe cases - Management: - Improve drainage; convert from flood to drip irrigation - Maintain graft union 15+ cm above soil line - Apply metalaxyl (Ridomil) as soil drench: 2 g/L around trunk base, twice per year (spring + autumn) - Phosphonate (fosetyl-Al) trunk injections for severely affected trees - Use tolerant rootstocks: Trifoliate orange, Citrumelo Swingle

    3. Gummosis (التصمغ): - Pathogen: Primarily Phytophthora citrophthora and P. parasitica - Symptoms: Amber gum exudation from trunk and scaffold branches; bark cracking; lesions that girdle the trunk - Management: - Surgically remove infected bark (cut 2 cm into healthy tissue) - Apply copper-based paste (Bordeaux paste) to wound - Paint trunks with white latex paint + copper hydroxide to prevent infection - Avoid trunk wetting from irrigation — use drip emitters 30+ cm from trunk - Maintain dry trunk base; remove soil mounding around trunk

    Q3: When is the best time to plant citrus trees and what spacing should be used?¶

    EN:

    Zone Best Planting Window Avoid Planting Reason
    Lowland hot zones (Lahj, Abyan, Jazan) September–November April–August Extreme summer heat (>42°C) causes transplant shock
    Mid-elevation (Hadhramaut, Najran) October–December May–August Moderate winters favor root establishment
    Highland (Sana'a, Asir, Al-Baha) February–April November–January Frost risk in winter; spring warmth promotes growth

    Spacing recommendations:

    Citrus Type Spacing (m) Trees/Hectare Notes
    Acid lime / Lemon 5 × 5 400 Can be closer (4 × 4) with intensive pruning
    Sweet orange 6 × 6 278 Standard for rain-fed or drip-irrigated orchards

Irrigate immediately after planting (30–40 liters per tree) 6. Apply 10 cm mulch layer to conserve moisture and reduce soil temperature 7. Provide shade (palm frond shelter) for the first summer in lowland zones

    Q5: What is the recommended fertilization program for citrus trees at different growth stages?¶

    Nutrient Concentration Timing Deficiency Symptom
    Zinc (ZnSO₄) 3–5 g/L Spring flush, post-harvest Interveinal chlorosis on young leaves, small leaves

    Citrus Leaf Miner: - Biological: Release Ageniaspis citricola parasitoid (where available); conserve native parasitoids by avoiding broad-spectrum sprays during non-critical periods - Cultural: Manage flush timing; prune to synchronize new growth; irrigate/fertilize to avoid continuous flushing - Chemical (threshold: >50% of new shoots infested): Abamectin 1.8% EC (0.5 mL/L) or spinosad (0.3 mL/L) targeting new flush only; rotate active ingredients - Timing: Spray when new leaves are 1–2 cm long (before mines form); do NOT spray mature leaves

    Citrus Aphids (مَنّ): - Biological (primary approach): Conserve ladybird beetles (Coccinella, Hippodamia), lacewings (Chrysoperla), and syrphid fly larvae; release Aphidius colemani parasitoid where available - Cultural: Manage nitrogen to reduce excessive soft growth; ant control (ants protect aphids from predators) using sticky bands around trunks - Chemical (threshold: >25% of shoots infested with colonies): Imidacloprid soil drench (0.5 g a.i./tree for young trees, 1.5 g for mature); or acetamiprid foliar spray (0.25 g/L); or mineral oil 1.5% (suffocating, less disruptive to beneficials) - CTV risk: In areas with CTV, aggressive aphid control on sour orange rootstock is critical during spring flush

    Scale Insects (soft and armored): - Biological: Release Cryptolaemus montrouzieri (mealybug destroyer) or Metaphycus parasitoids; conserve natural enemies - Cultural: Prune to open canopy (improves air circulation, exposes scales to heat and predators); control ants - Chemical: Petroleum spray oil 2% (summer oil) during crawler emergence; spirotetramat (Movento) for systemic control; avoid organophosphates that kill beneficials
