    Vegetables Q&A: Yemen & Southern Saudi Arabia | أسئلة وأجوبة الخضروات: اليمن وجنوب السعودية¶

    Q1: What are the most important vegetable crops grown in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, and how are they ranked by production volume?¶

    EN: Tomato and potato consistently rank as the top two vegetable crops in Yemen and the southern regions of Saudi Arabia (Jazan, Asir, Najran). Together they account for over 50% of total vegetable production. The full ranking by approximate annual production in Yemen is:

    Rank Crop Arabic Name Est. Production (Yemen) Key Growing Areas
    1 Tomato طماطم 250,000–300,000 t/yr Tihama, Sana'a basin, Lahij, Abyan
    2 Potato بطاطس 180,000–220,000 t/yr Dhamar, Amran, Sa'dah highlands
    3 Onion بصل 80,000–100,000 t/yr Marib, Hadramawt, Al-Jawf
    4 Watermelon بطيخ أحمر 60,000–80,000 t/yr Tihama wadis, Abyan delta
    5 Okra باميا 30,000–40,000 t/yr Tihama, Jazan, Lahij
    6 Pepper (chili & sweet) فلفل 25,000–35,000 t/yr Tihama, Ibb, Dhamar
    7 Cucumber خيار 20,000–30,000 t/yr Highland greenhouses, Jazan

    In southern Saudi Arabia, Jazan Province is the primary vegetable-producing region, with tomato, okra, watermelon, and leafy greens dominating, while Asir highlands produce potato, onion, and cool-season vegetables. These crops form the backbone of household food security and local market supply for millions of smallholder farming families.

    Q2: How do vegetable growing seasons differ between the Tihama coastal lowlands and the highlands of Yemen and Asir?¶

    EN: The dramatic elevation gradient — from sea level on the Tihama coast to 2,500+ meters in the highlands — creates two fundamentally different vegetable calendars:

    Factor Tihama Lowlands (0–500 m) Highlands (1,500–2,800 m)
    Temperature range 25–42°C summer, 18–30°C winter 5–25°C summer, -2–18°C winter
    Main vegetable season October–March (cool/dry) March–September (warm/rainy)
    Off-season challenge Extreme heat (June–Sept), >40°C Frost risk (Dec–Feb), below 0°C
    Rainfall <100 mm/yr; spate irrigation from wadis 400–800 mm/yr; rainfed + supplemental
    Dominant crops Okra, watermelon, tomato, chili, eggplant Potato, onion, garlic, cabbage, carrot
    Irrigation method Flood/basin from wadi spate; drip expanding Furrow, basin; sprinkler for potato
    Growing cycle Short cycles (60–90 days); 2–3 crops/year Single main season; some double-cropping
    Pest pressure Very high (whitefly, fruit fly, aphids year-round) Moderate; late blight in wet years

    Tihama (including Jazan): Farmers plant heat-tolerant varieties of tomato and pepper in late September–October, harvesting December–February for peak market prices. Okra and watermelon follow from November through April. Summer plantings are risky without shade or greenhouse protection due to temperatures exceeding 42°C and extreme pest pressure.

    Highlands (Dhamar, Amran, Ibb, Asir): The main season begins with spring rains in March. Potato is planted March–April and harvested July–August. A second potato crop may be planted in August for a November harvest. Cool-season crops like onion, garlic, cabbage, and carrot dominate from September through February, taking advantage of mild autumn temperatures.

    المرتفعات (ذمار، عمران، إب، عسير): يبدأ الموسم الرئيسي مع أمطار الربيع في مارس. تُزرع البطاطس في مارس–أبريل وتُحصد في يوليو–أغسطس. يمكن زراعة محصول ثانٍ في أغسطس لحصاد نوفمبر. تسود محاصيل الموسم البارد كالبصل والثوم والملفوف والجزر من سبتمبر حتى فبراير مستفيدةً من اعتدال درجات حرارة الخريف.

    Q3: What are the recommended tomato varieties for Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, and how is Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) managed?¶

    EN: Tomato is the single most important vegetable crop in the region. Variety selection and TYLCV management are the two most critical decisions a tomato farmer makes.

    Recommended Varieties:

    Category Varieties Characteristics Best Region
    Open-pollinated (local) Baqouri (بقوري), Baladi Sana'a, Maris Heat-tolerant, good flavor, lower yield Tihama, highland home gardens
    Hybrid (determinate) GS-12, Strain-B, Super Red Compact, uniform, 40–60 t/ha Tihama field production
    Hybrid (indeterminate) Carmen, Dafnis, Lara F1 High yield (80–120 t/ha), need staking Highland fields, greenhouses
    TYLCV-resistant Ty-1 lines: Saladette, TY Faina, Shanty F1 Carry Ty-1/Ty-2 resistance genes All whitefly-prone areas
    Heat-tolerant hybrid Nema 1400, HT-36, Solar Set Set fruit above 35°C, moderate TYLCV tolerance Tihama summer, Jazan
    Cherry/processing Shiren F1, Roma VF Dual-purpose, disease-tolerant Greenhouse, export

    TYLCV Management — Integrated Approach:

    TYLCV, transmitted by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci (biotype B/MEAM1), is the most devastating tomato disease in Tihama, Jazan, and lowland Yemen. Yield losses can reach 100% without management.

    Management Layer Practice Timing
    Resistant varieties Plant Ty-1/Ty-2 gene carriers exclusively in hotspot areas Variety selection (pre-season)
    Nursery protection 50-mesh insect-proof netting over seedling trays Nursery stage (0–30 days)
    Reflective mulch Silver/aluminum reflective mulch repels whitefly At transplanting
    Yellow sticky traps 40 traps/ha for monitoring; mass trapping at field edges From transplanting onward
    Chemical control Imidacloprid drench at transplanting; rotate with pyriproxyfen, spiromesifen, cyantraniliprole Weeks 1–6 after transplanting
    Biological control Release Eretmocerus mundus or Encarsia formosa parasitoids Weeks 3–8, greenhouse preferred
    Roguing Remove and burn plants showing TYLCV symptoms (curling, yellowing, stunting) within 14 days of infection Throughout season
    Crop-free period Maintain 2–3 month tomato-free window between seasons Off-season (June–August in Tihama)

    Critical Rule: Never plant a new tomato crop adjacent to an existing infected crop. Maintain at least 500 m separation or a temporal gap of 8+ weeks.

    Q4: How is potato cultivated in the Yemeni highlands (Dhamar, Amran), and what varieties and practices are recommended?¶

    EN: Potato is Yemen's second most important vegetable crop and the primary cash crop for highland farmers between 1,800–2,600 m elevation. Dhamar Governorate alone produces roughly 40% of Yemen's total potato output.

    Growing Seasons:

    Season Planting Harvest Region Notes
    Spring (main) Feb–Mar Jun–Jul Dhamar, Amran, Sana'a, Asir Largest production window
    Summer Apr–May Aug–Sep Sa'dah, high Dhamar (>2,200 m) Cooler microclimates only
    Autumn (second crop) Aug–Sep Nov–Dec Dhamar, Amran Using retained seed from spring
    Winter Oct–Nov Feb–Mar Lower highlands (1,500–1,800 m), wadi margins Frost risk, limited area

    Recommended Varieties:

    Variety Origin Maturity Yield Potential Key Traits
    Désirée Netherlands Medium (90–110 d) 25–35 t/ha Red skin, drought-tolerant, late blight moderate resistance; most popular in Yemen
    Spunta Netherlands Medium-early (80–100 d) 30–40 t/ha Yellow flesh, high yield, good storage; dominant in Dhamar
    Diamant Netherlands Medium (90–110 d) 25–35 t/ha White flesh, excellent cooking quality, good in Amran
    Arinda Netherlands Early (75–90 d) 20–30 t/ha Early bulking, heat-tolerant; suitable for lower elevations
    Local selections Yemen Variable 15–20 t/ha Adapted, disease-tolerant, lower yield but familiar

    Key Cultivation Practices:

    1. Seed tuber quality: Use certified seed (G3–G4 generation) imported or from highland seed multiplication programs. Cut tubers to 40–60 g pieces with 2–3 eyes. Treat cut surfaces with fungicide (mancozeb) and allow 24–48 hr healing before planting.

    2. Land preparation: Deep plowing (30–40 cm) on terraced fields. Incorporate 15–20 t/ha well-decomposed farmyard manure (FYM). Form ridges 75 cm apart, 25–30 cm high.

    3. Planting: Space seed pieces 25–30 cm apart in furrows, 10–15 cm deep. Plant density: 44,000–53,000 plants/ha.

    4. Fertilization: Basal application of 150–200 kg/ha DAP (diammonium phosphate) + 100–150 kg/ha potassium sulfate. Side-dress with 150–200 kg/ha urea in two splits (at emergence and at hilling).

    5. Irrigation: Furrow irrigation every 7–10 days. Critical periods: stolon initiation (30–45 DAP) and tuber bulking (45–75 DAP). Deficit at these stages reduces yield by 30–50%.

    6. Hilling: Two hillings — first at 15–20 cm plant height, second 2–3 weeks later. Essential to prevent tuber greening and sunscald.

    7. Late blight management (Phytophthora infestans): The most serious potato disease in wet highland seasons. Apply preventive fungicides (mancozeb 2.5 g/L) starting at canopy closure. Switch to systemic (metalaxyl + mancozeb) at first symptoms. Spray every 7–10 days during rainy periods.

    8. Storage: Traditional underground pits (matmura/مطمورة) lined with straw store seed potatoes 3–6 months at highland temperatures (10–15°C). Diffused light storage (DLS) warehouses reduce sprouting and maintain seed vigor.

    سبونتا مبكر-متوسط (80–100 يوم) 30–40 طن/هـ لحم أصفر، إنتاج عالٍ، تخزين جيد؛ سائد في ذمار
    ديامانت متوسط (90–110 يوم) 25–35 طن/هـ لحم أبيض، جودة طبخ ممتازة، جيد في عمران
    أريندا مبكر (75–90 يوم) 20–30 طن/هـ تبكير في التدرن، متحمل للحرارة؛ مناسب للارتفاعات المنخفضة

    الممارسات الزراعية الرئيسية:

    1. جودة التقاوي: استخدام تقاوٍ معتمدة (جيل G3–G4). تقطيع الدرنات إلى قطع 40–60 غ مع 2–3 عيون. معاملة الأسطح المقطوعة بمبيد فطري (مانكوزيب) وترك 24–48 ساعة للتجفيف.

    2. تحضير الأرض: حراثة عميقة (30–40 سم) على المدرجات. إضافة 15–20 طن/هـ سماد بلدي متحلل. تكوين خطوط بعرض 75 سم وارتفاع 25–30 سم.

    3. الزراعة: مسافة 25–30 سم بين القطع في الأخاديد، بعمق 10–15 سم. كثافة: 44,000–53,000 نبات/هـ.

    4. التسميد: تسميد أساسي 150–200 كغ/هـ DAP + 100–150 كغ/هـ سلفات بوتاسيوم. تسميد جانبي 150–200 كغ/هـ يوريا على دفعتين.

    5. الري: ري بالأخاديد كل 7–10 أيام. الفترات الحرجة: بدء تكوين الاستولونات والتدرّن. النقص في هذه المراحل يقلل المحصول 30–50%.

    6. التكويم (الترديم): تكويمتان — الأولى عند ارتفاع 15–20 سم والثانية بعد 2–3 أسابيع. ضروري لمنع اخضرار الدرنات.

    7. إدارة الفحة المتأخرة: أخطر أمراض البطاطس في المواسم الممطرة. رش وقائي بمانكوزيب (2.5 غ/ل) بدءاً من إغلاق المجموع الخضري. التحول إلى جهازي (ميتالاكسيل + مانكوزيب) عند أول أعراض. الرش كل 7–10 أيام خلال فترات المطر.

    8. التخزين: الحفر التقليدية تحت الأرض (المطمورة) المبطنة بالقش تخزّن تقاوي البطاطس 3–6 أشهر في درجات حرارة المرتفعات (10–15°م). مستودعات الضوء المنتشر (DLS) تقلل التنبيت وتحافظ على حيوية التقاوي.

    Q5: What are the best practices for onion cultivation in Marib, Hadramawt, and the southern regions?¶

    EN: Onion is the third most important vegetable in Yemen, with Marib, Al-Jawf, and Hadramawt as the leading production zones due to their dry climate, which reduces fungal diseases and promotes good curing and long storage. In Saudi Arabia, the Najran and Asir regions are significant producers.

    Varieties and Types:

    Type Varieties Bulb Characteristics Storage Life Best Areas
    Red (local) Beri Yemeni (بيري يمني), Marib Red Dark red, pungent, medium-large 4–6 months Marib, Hadramawt, Al-Jawf
    White Texas Early White, Granex types Mild, sweet, large 2–3 months Southern Saudi, export
    Yellow Giza 20, Giza 6 (Egyptian) Medium pungency, firm 3–5 months Wadi Hadramawt
    Short-day adapted Red Creole C-5, Mercedes Bulbs under 11–12 hr daylength 3–4 months All lowland areas (<1,000 m)

    Seasonal Calendar (Marib/lowland):

    Activity Timing Details
    Nursery sowing September–October Raised beds, 5 g seed/m², shade cloth
    Transplanting November–December 45–60 day-old seedlings, 15 cm × 10 cm spacing
    Bulb initiation January–February Triggered by increasing daylength (>11 hr)
    Harvest March–April When 50–75% of tops have fallen
    Curing April Field-cure 7–10 days under shade, then store

    Key Practices for Marib and Dry Regions:

    1. Water management: Onion requires consistent moisture during bulb expansion (Jan–Feb) but must be cut off 2–3 weeks before harvest. Over-irrigation causes thick necks and poor storage. Deficit irrigation in the final month increases pungency and storage life.

    2. Fertilization: Apply 120–150 kg N/ha (split into 3 applications: transplant, 30 DAT, 60 DAT), 80–100 kg P₂O₅/ha (basal), and 100–120 kg K₂O/ha (basal + 1 side-dress). Sulfur application (30–40 kg S/ha) improves flavor and disease resistance.

    3. Weed management: Onion is an exceptionally poor competitor against weeds due to its upright, narrow leaves. Pre-emergence herbicide (pendimethalin 1.5 L/ha) followed by 2–3 hand weedings is standard practice.

    4. Disease management: Purple blotch (Alternaria porri) is the most common disease. Manage with mancozeb or chlorothalonil sprays every 10–14 days during humid periods. Avoid overhead irrigation.

    5. Curing and storage: Harvest when necks are soft and tops begin drying. Cure in the field under shade for 7–10 days until outer skins are papery and necks fully sealed. Store in well-ventilated structures at ambient temperature (Marib's dry heat of 25–35°C is ideal). Properly cured Marib onions store 4–6 months.

    Wadi Cultivation System:

    The traditional wadi cultivation system is remarkable for its water efficiency:

    1. Site selection: Choose wadi terraces or floodplain margins where silt has deposited after seasonal floods (kharif). Soil should be deep alluvial silt-loam with good moisture retention.

    2. Hole planting (Jurah/جورة): Dig planting holes 40–50 cm wide × 30 cm deep at 2–3 m spacing. Mix excavated soil with 2–3 kg FYM per hole. This concentrates fertility and moisture around each plant.

    3. Moisture management: In pure rainfed wadi systems, planting occurs as floodwaters recede. Residual soil moisture at 60–100 cm depth sustains the crop. Where wells exist, 1–2 supplemental irrigations during fruit set and enlargement dramatically increase yield (from 15 to 30+ t/ha).

    4. Mulching: Spread a thin layer of gravel or dry grass around plants to reduce evaporation — critical in environments with daily ET > 8 mm.

    5. Pollination: Bee activity is essential. Avoid insecticide application during morning flowering hours (6–10 AM). Place 2–3 beehives per hectare near the field if available.

    6. Harvest indicators: Watermelon — tendril near fruit attachment dries; ground spot turns cream-yellow; hollow sound when tapped. Melon — slip stage (stem separates with light pressure); strong aroma; netting fully developed.

    Special Focus — Tuta absoluta Management:

    Tuta absoluta, first detected in Yemen around 2015 and now established across the region, requires a dedicated multi-layered approach:

    1. Mass trapping: Install delta pheromone traps at 20–25 per hectare (40+ per hectare for high-pressure areas). Check and replace lures every 4–6 weeks.
    2. Water traps: Light traps with soapy water basins attract and drown adult moths at night. Place 5–10 per hectare along field edges.
    3. Biological: Release Nesidiocoris tenuis at 1–2 adults/m² at transplanting. This mirid bug is both a predator of Tuta eggs/larvae and whitefly, making it the most cost-effective biocontrol agent in the region.
    4. Bt sprays: Apply B. thuringiensis var. kurstaki (Btk) at 1–1.5 L/ha every 7 days when larval counts exceed 1 larva per 5 leaves.
    5. Chemical rotation: Chlorantraniliprole (Coragen) → Spinosad → Emamectin benzoate → Indoxacarb. Never apply the same MoA group more than 2 consecutive times.
    6. Sanitation: Remove and destroy (burn or deeply bury) all infested plant material. Do not compost.

    TYLCV Management — Integrated Approach:

    TYLCV, transmitted by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci (biotype B/MEAM1), is the most devastating tomato disease in the region, causing 40–100% yield loss in unprotected fields. An integrated management program includes:

    1. Resistant/tolerant varieties: Use cultivars carrying Ty-1, Ty-2, or Ty-3 resistance genes (e.g., CLN2498E, TY-52). This is the most cost-effective control measure.
    2. Whitefly exclusion: Use 50-mesh insect-proof nets on nurseries and greenhouse openings. Raise seedlings under net protection for the first 30 days.
    3. Reflective mulch: Silver-colored plastic mulch repels whitefly and reduces landing rates by 50–70%.
    4. Yellow sticky traps: Deploy 20–30 yellow sticky traps per hectare for monitoring and mass trapping. Replace every 7–10 days.
    5. Chemical control: Rotate insecticide groups to prevent resistance buildup:
    6. Neonicotinoids (imidacloprid) as seedling drench at transplanting
    7. Pyriproxyfen (IGR) targeting nymphs
    8. Spiromesifen or cyantraniliprole for adults
    9. Avoid pyrethroids (whitefly resistance is widespread)
    10. Crop-free period: Maintain a 6–8 week tomato-free period between seasons in Tihama to break the virus cycle.
    11. Remove infected plants: Rogue and destroy plants showing TYLCV symptoms (leaf curling, yellowing, stunting) within the first 3 weeks after transplanting.

    Key Cultivation Practices:

    • Seed tubers: Import certified seed from the Netherlands or Egypt. Local seed degenerates after 2–3 generations due to virus accumulation. Informal seed systems account for 70% of planted seed and are a major yield-limiting factor.
    • Land preparation: Deep plowing (25–30 cm) on terraced highland fields. Ridge planting at 60–75 cm row spacing, 25–30 cm within-row spacing.
    • Fertilization: Apply 150–200 kg/ha DAP (18-46-0) at planting and 150 kg/ha urea (46-0-0) as side-dress at tuber initiation (30–40 days after planting).
    • Irrigation: Primarily rain-fed in summer season; supplementary furrow irrigation from springs or cisterns in spring season. Total water requirement: 400–500 mm per crop cycle.
    • Major diseases: Late blight (Phytophthora infestans) is the most serious threat, particularly in years with above-average rainfall. Apply preventive mancozeb (2 kg/ha) every 10–14 days during wet periods, switching to metalaxyl + mancozeb (Ridomil Gold) if disease is detected.
    • Storage: Traditional underground pit storage (matmura/مطمورة) is common but causes 20–30% losses. Cold storage at 4°C with 90% humidity extends shelf life to 4–6 months.

    Q6: How is okra (الباميا) cultivated in the Tihama plain and Jazan region, and what makes it well-suited to these areas?¶

    EN: Okra (Abelmoschus esculentus) is one of the most reliable and profitable vegetable crops in the Tihama lowlands of Yemen and the Jazan region of Saudi Arabia. Its exceptional heat tolerance (thriving at 30–40°C), short time to first harvest (45–55 days), and extended picking season (3–5 months) make it ideally suited to hot, low-altitude environments where many other vegetables fail.

    Low input cost Minimal fertilizer and pesticide needs
    Market demand Strong daily demand in local markets for fresh okra
    Cultural importance Staple ingredient in Yemeni and Saudi cuisine (bamia stew)

    Cultivation Practices:

    • Sowing: Direct seeding in October–November (Tihama) or September–October (Jazan). Soak seeds in water for 12–24 hours before sowing to break dormancy and improve germination from 60% to 85%.
    • Spacing: 60–80 cm between rows, 30–40 cm between plants. Thin to one plant per station after emergence.
    • Irrigation: Furrow irrigation every 5–7 days. Total water requirement: 300–450 mm per season. Drip irrigation reduces water use by 40% and improves yield quality.
    • Fertilization: Apply 80 kg/ha urea + 100 kg/ha DAP at planting; side-dress 50 kg/ha urea at first flowering (30–35 days). Avoid excessive nitrogen which promotes vegetative growth over pod production.
    • Harvesting: Pick pods every 2–3 days when 6–10 cm long and still tender. Delayed harvest by even 1–2 days results in tough, fibrous pods that lose market value. Harvest early morning when pods snap cleanly.
    • Key pest: Jassids (leafhoppers) cause leaf curling; control with neem oil or imidacloprid. Shoot and fruit borer (Earias spp.) damages pods; handpick affected pods and apply Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt).

    • تكلفة طاقة التبريد: التبريد التبخيري في جازان يستهلك كهرباء كبيرة؛ الأنظمة الشمسية ناشئة.
    • الخبرة التقنية: نقص في مشغلي البيوت المحمية المدربين.
    • الملوحة: ملوحة المياه الجوفية في ساحل جازان (2,000–5,000 جزء بالمليون) تتطلب تحلية.

    Q9: What are the most damaging vegetable pests in Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia, and how are they managed through integrated pest management (IPM)?¶

    EN: The warm climate of Yemen and southern Saudi Arabia creates year-round pest pressure on vegetable crops. The following table ranks the most economically damaging pests, several of which are invasive species that have become established in the region within the last two decades.

    Key IPM Principles for the Region:

    1. Never rely on chemicals alone: Resistance develops rapidly in whitefly and Tuta absoluta. Always combine 3+ IPM components.
    2. Rotate chemical groups: Never apply the same insecticide group more than 2 consecutive times. Use IRAC mode-of-action codes for rotation planning.
    3. Protect natural enemies: Avoid broad-spectrum pyrethroids and organophosphates which destroy parasitoids and predators.
    4. Nursery protection is critical: Most TYLCV infection occurs in the first 30 days. Protect seedlings under nets from day one.
    5. Regional coordination: Tuta absoluta and whitefly do not respect farm boundaries. Community-wide adoption of crop-free periods and trap networks is essential.
